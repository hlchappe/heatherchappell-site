# 00 · Start Here
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page tells you what SIDE EYE research is, who decides what, and where to go next. Read it first, whether you are a human vendor, a connected AI agent or an outside AI tool working from a pasted pack.

## What SIDE EYE research is

SIDE EYE is a history magazine and a YouTube show made by Heather and Edwin. The tagline is "History, allegedly." Most topics are popular conspiracy stories. Our job is to find out which parts the record supports, which parts it does not, and how sure we can be about each one.

Research starts with the magazine. Most magazine topics later become a YouTube video, so one research package is built to serve both formats (decision DEC-2026-001). The package moves through ten gates, R0 to R9. Gates R0 to R8 produce the magazine package. Gate R9 extends the same package to a video. Page 01 (The Research Process) sets out every gate.

## The one rule

**Test the conspiracy. Don't assume it.**

Every pitch enters as a question marked "untested." A popular claim is a lead to check. It is never a fact to build on. When the record says no, the story changes to fit the record.

What follows from the rule:

- Never invent a quote, fact, source or detail.
- Rate every fact on the six-level scale (page 02, How Sure Are We?).
- Never raise a rating above its evidence. When two files disagree, the lower rating wins until a ruling is logged.
- Our own reports, scripts, magazine pages and any AI output are never sources. They are leads.
- Never get past a site that blocks you. Log it and follow page 11 (Access and Blocked Sources).
- Never mark anything APPROVED or LOCKED.

## How to ask a question

Most questions already have an answer. Look in this order:

1. The topic's rulings file and Must NOT list.
2. The Decision Register and the Rulings Index (page 09).
3. The FAQ (page 12).
4. Still open? Add a row to the package's question file with:
   - the question
   - where you looked
   - the options and what each changes
   - your proposed answer

Until the question is ruled, use the lower rating and keep working. The Research Lead batches open rows into one decision-needed page at Heather's next gate. Questions reach Heather only through her decisions channel, each with 2 to 4 options, a pick, examples, a deadline and a priority (page 09, How a question reaches Heather). The answer goes into the rulings file, the Rulings Index and the Decision Register the same day. If it changes a role card or an AI file, that file is updated too.

Vendors never message Heather directly about research. Agents never ask Heather a question the Decision Register already answers.

# 01 · The Research Process
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page sets out the one research process every SIDE EYE topic follows, from first question to a checked package ready for the magazine and the video. Use it to know which gate you are in, what you must hand in, and when a gate has passed.

## Magazine first, one package for both formats

Research starts with the magazine. Most topics then become a YouTube video, so the foundation research is built to serve both (decision DEC-2026-001).

- Gates R0 to R8 produce the magazine package. R0 names the magazine first and notes whether a video is likely. R8 hands off the magazine pack plus video-ready notes.
- Gate R9 (YouTube extension) builds the video from the same Fact Ledger. There is no fresh research round for facts the package already holds.
- No script, layout or art may use a fact until that fact has a ledger row, a rating and a check.

One Fact Ledger per topic is the master. When the magazine and the video disagree, neither wins: the ledger is fixed, and every product that used the fact is fixed from it (page 04, Checking and Corrections).

## Tiers

The Research Lead sets the tier at R0 and writes it in the research log.

| Tier | Covers | Gates it runs |
|---|---|---|
| A | Lead features, Case Files, covers | Every gate, R0 to R8, plus R9 when a video follows |
| B | Sidebars, and Shorts cut from approved work | R1, R4 and R7, against an existing ledger |
| C | Opinion pieces and host banter | R7 on any fact it states |

Shorts are regular horizontal videos longer than 60 seconds (DEC-2026-019). They are checked like any other product.

## The gates

Each gate lists who leads, what must be true before it starts, the steps, what must be true for it to pass, Heather's part, and what it produces. A gate passes only when every "passes when" item is true and filed in Drive. Nothing counts as delivered until it is in Drive.

### R0 · Intake

- **Enters when:** a topic is named.
- **Steps:**
  1. Write the pitch as a testable question with names, places and years. Mark it "untested."
  2. Name the formats: magazine first, and note whether a video is likely.
  3. Set the tier, the owner (Research Lead) and the search budget.
  4. Set the living-person risk tier: Green (dead or historical), Amber (living public figure, documented facts only) or Red (a living person plus crime, fraud, sex, health, coordination or money-for-favors, or any private person). See page 05.
  5. Open the research log and give the package its ID (RP-###).
- **Passes when:** the question is testable, formats and tier are set, the log is open and the package has an ID.
- **Heather:** approves the question. Edwin is consulted.
- **Outputs:** research log, package folder, Research Register row.

### R1 · Inventory and brief

- **Enters when:** R0 has passed.
- **Steps:**
  1. Read and log what already exists: Drive research, sources docs, corrections, the Decision Register, the Killed Claims list and the Source Access Registry.
  2. Write the common brief: the question, facts already established (with ratings), the rules, the rating scale, the report format, the search budget, known blocked sites, and every Must NOT line that applies.
  3. Reserve the checker's budget now: one search per print-bound fact expected, plus 10, to be run in a fresh session.
- **Passes when:** the inventory is logged, the brief is filed and frozen, and the checker reserve is written in the log.
- **Heather:** none.
- **Outputs:** inventory list, common brief.

### R2 · Gather

- **Enters when:** the brief is frozen.
- **Steps:**
  1. The Research Lead launches one agent or vendor per desk (a desk covers one set of people, years and questions).
  2. Each desk returns a report in the standard format: summary of 200 to 450 words, rated findings with source chains, links table, leads, and an access-needed list with full citations.
  3. Blocked sources go on the access list and into the Source Access Registry.
  4. The Lead writes the Phase 1 summary.
- **Passes when:** every desk report is in the standard format and filed in 01 Desk Reports, and the Phase 1 summary is filed.
- **Heather:** none.
- **Outputs:** desk reports, links tables, access list, Phase 1 summary.

### R3 · Gaps and books

- **Enters when:** the Phase 1 summary shows thin areas or access needs. If it shows none, log "R3 not needed" with the reason.
- **Steps:**
  1. Send gap teams to the thin areas. Log every correction they make to Phase 1 findings.
  2. Rank the books and sources on the access list.
  3. Send blocked-site requests to Heather (DEC-2026-003). Batch them for this gate when the work can wait.
  4. Assign one reader per book chunk. Every finding carries a page and line.
- **Passes when:** gap corrections are logged and every access item is marked Got, Ordered or Waived.
- **Heather:** chooses which books to get. Opens blocked sites in her browser when asked; each one is logged in the Source Access Registry.
- **Outputs:** gap reports, reader reports, updated access list.

### R4 · Ledger and readiness

- **Enters when:** R2 has passed, and R3 if it ran.
- **Steps:**
  1. Build one Fact Ledger with a fact ID per row: statement with its hedge (safe wording), level, source chain, quote level, check status, last changed, used in.
  2. Build the conflict register. Where sources or files disagree, the lower rating wins until a ruling is logged.
  3. Run the readiness check: a sourced popular belief to test, at least one clock (a date or deadline that drives the story), at least one readable document, and at least 10 surprises rated Proven.
- **Passes when:** the ledger is complete, every conflict is ruled or held at the lower rating, and the readiness check passes.
- **Heather:** rules on flagged conflicts that would change the thesis, a title word or a locked doc. The Lead rules on wording-level conflicts.
- **Outputs:** Fact Ledger (sheet), conflict register, rulings file.

### R5 · Claims and attack

- **Enters when:** R4 has passed.
- **Steps:**
  1. List five or six claims. Fix the proof standard for each one before any attack starts.
  2. Launch one red-team agent per claim. Score the central claim, never the true facts around it.
  3. Run the myth-buster: the strongest case against the thesis, unsoftened.
  4. Write the scorecard: each claim rated, corrections to carry, before-print checks ranked by value.
  5. Write the Must NOT list. Each line gets its reason and fact ID, and goes into the Killed Claims list.
- **Passes when:** every claim has a red-team result, the scorecard and Must NOT list are filed, and the Killed Claims list is updated.
- **Heather:** none. Edwin is consulted.
- **Outputs:** claims and proof standards, red-team reports, myth-buster report, scorecard, Must NOT list.

### R6 · Thesis and drill

- **Enters when:** the R5 scorecard is filed.
- **Steps:**
  1. Write the thesis as "Everybody thinks X. The record shows Y." with four or five supporting points, each with its level.
  2. Run the surprise drill: one specialist per strong lead, about 30 searches each. Or log a waiver.
  3. Update the rulings file so the checker has it before R7.
- **Passes when:** the thesis and points are filed with levels, and the drill is run or waived in the log.
- **Heather:** approves the thesis. The title, cover line and headline are written after the thesis is approved, never before. Edwin is consulted.
- **Outputs:** thesis page, drill reports.

### R7 · Independent check

- **Enters when:** there is a numbered list of every print-bound fact, plus the cover, headline and teaser. The rulings file is current. The checker reserve is unspent.
- **Steps:**
  1. A checker who did no research on the topic and wrote nothing in the piece runs the check in a fresh session.
  2. Check the mandatory set first: cover, title, headline, teaser, craziest part, biggest number, twist, other-side quote, every direct quote, every number, every claim about a living person.
  3. Record each result in the fact-check log: Confirmed, Confirmed with correction, Not confirmed, Wrong or Unchecked.
  4. Run the legal gate checklist. Someone who did not write the piece runs it (DEC-2026-005).
  5. Fix and re-check. At most two fix loops. A third goes to Heather with both reports.
- **Passes when:** every item has a result. The mandatory set has zero Wrong and zero Not confirmed. Open items are zero, or each one is signed in the Decision Register. Any fact still unchecked is hedged, cut or held, and never printed at its old rating.
- **Heather:** signs any Not confirmed result that is kept outside the mandatory set.
- **Outputs:** fact-check log, legal gate checklist, rating-change rows.

### R8 · Package and hand-off

- **Enters when:** R7 has passed.
- **Steps:**
  1. File the package: Fact Ledger, scorecard, Must NOT list, rulings, sources doc by section, access list, fact-check log and research log.
  2. Write the cover sheet with counts: checked, unchecked, UNREAD, blocked sites, AI tools used and for what.
  3. Build the magazine pack: the What We Can Prove / What We Can't box and the other side.
  4. Write video-ready notes: quotes with their quote levels, documents, places and visuals a script will need.
  5. Write the handoff note in the research log.
- **Passes when:** every item above is filed in 04 Package and the Research Register row is updated.
- **Heather:** accepts the package and sets it APPROVED.
- **Outputs:** the approved package, magazine pack, video-ready notes.

### R9 · YouTube extension

- **Enters when:** R8 has passed and a video is going ahead.
- **Steps:**
  1. Fill Template A from the same Fact Ledger.
  2. List what the video needs that the magazine did not: on-screen quote cards (Q1 or Q2 only), footage and image sources, the cold open, the withheld object. Each one goes in as a change request on the same ledger and is checked independently.
  3. Heather approves the video thesis.
  4. Run a light fact pass at outline stage, before the script is near final.
  5. Write the title and thumbnail after the script (DEC-2026-018). Rate them on the six-level scale and check them like the body.
- **Passes when:** every air-bound fact, the title and the thumbnail have a check result.
- **Heather:** approves the video thesis, then the title and thumbnail.
- **Outputs:** Template A dossier, change requests with check results, video fact-check log.

## Change requests

Any new fact found during scripting, art, layout or R9 goes through a change request before anyone uses it.

Fields:

- CR ID, date, raised by
- Product and where the fact would run (page, timecode, title, thumbnail, Short, description)
- The new fact or change, with its source chain
- Proposed level
- Fact ID of the new or changed ledger row
- Checker result and date
- Status: Open, Checked, Rejected

A change request that changes a rating, a claim or the thesis is a major change (page 09). Heather rules on any that touch the thesis or a title word.

## Waivers

A gate or step may be skipped only with a waiver line in the research log:

- gate and step skipped
- reason
- guardrail put in place (hedge, hold, cut)
- who approved it, and the date

R7 is never waived for a print-bound or air-bound fact.

## Agent operations

Every agent and vendor should know these limits.

- **The lead launches every agent.** Agents cannot launch agents. The Research Lead starts each desk, red-team, myth-buster and checker agent directly, in parallel where possible.
- **Search caps.** The allowance is shared and runs out. 30 searches per desk agent. 15 for red team and quick checks. About 200 for all of Phase 1. The checker reserve set at R1 is never spent on other work.
- **Prefer known pages.** Fetch known pages and full texts before running new searches.
- **Return length.** Full report to file. Summary of 200 to 450 words (350 for red team).
- **Quote pieces.** The fetch tool can return quotes in pieces of 125 characters or fewer. Quote only the exact piece, mark every cut, and recheck any quote rebuilt from pieces as one whole quote before it is used.
- **Fix loops.** At most two fix loops after a failed check. A third goes to Heather with both reports.
- **Blocked sites.** Never get past a block. Log it in the Source Access Registry and ask for Heather's browser through the Lead (page 11).
- **Scope.** Work only in the folders your brief names.
- **Session end.** Close every session with a handoff note in the research log.

# 02 · How Sure Are We? The Rating Scale
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page holds the one rating scale for every SIDE EYE fact, claim, title and thumbnail, and the rules for how each level may be said on video, in print and in packaging. If any older doc, template or skill uses another label, convert it with the mapping table below (decision DEC-2026-002).

## The six levels

Rate every fact and every claim on these six levels. Score a claim on its central assertion, never on the true facts around it.

| Level | Meaning | Minimum source |
|---|---|---|
| Proven | A primary document seen, or two or more independent chains agree | Tier 1 or 2, checked |
| Strongly supported | One sound scholarly chain citing a primary we have not seen, or a small gap | Tier 3 |
| Likely | One good source or a strong inference, with nothing against it | Tier 3 or 4 |
| Contested | Sources disagree, or the claim rests on a self-interested, late or single source | Any |
| Unsupported | Claimed somewhere; no evidence found | None |
| Refuted | Documents contradict it | Tier 1 to 3 |

Source tiers (1 primary, 2 published edition, 3 scholarly secondary, 4 press and popular history, 5 wikis, 6 AI and our own work) are set out in page 03 (Sources and Quotes). Tier 5 and 6 never support a level alone. Count independent chains. Ten writers quoting one historian are one chain.

## Two flags

UNREAD and CHECK are flags. They sit beside a level and never replace it.

| Flag | Means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| UNREAD | The source is cited but blocked or unreachable | Keep the last rating. Add a guardrail in copy (a hedge or an A/B line). Log the source in the Source Access Registry (page 11). |
| CHECK | An open item | Close it or have it signed off before lock. Open CHECKs must be zero, or each signed by name, before a deliverable counts as verified (page 04). |

## Rules that apply to every rating

**Split rule.** When a source reports a speech or a view, rate the attribution and the content on separate lines. "The biography quotes the speaker saying X" and "The speaker said X" are two facts with two levels.

**Lower grade wins.** When two files give one fact different levels, use the lower one until a ruling is logged in the rulings file and the Rulings Index. Never settle it by picking the file you like.

**Never raise a rating without evidence.** A rating goes up only with a new source, logged as a rating-change row (fact ID, old level, new level, date, reason, evidence, who). Intensifiers that push a Likely toward Proven ("definitely", "clearly", "it is a fact that") count as raising it.

**Packaging is rated.** Titles, thumbnails, covers, cover lines, headlines, teasers, descriptions, pinned comments and Shorts go through the same scale and the same independent check as the body.

**Check results are separate.** Confirmed, Confirmed with correction, Not confirmed, Wrong and Unchecked are check results. MUST, SHOULD and NOTE are fix severities. FACT, DRIFT and EAR are route codes. None of these is a level.

## How each level is said

| Level | Spoken on video or podcast | Magazine body | Title, thumbnail, cover, teaser |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proven | Said flat | Said flat | Allowed |
| Strongly supported | Said flat, with the source named | Same | Allowed, with the source named in the body (proposed default, not yet approved) |
| Likely | Hedged in the same sentence ("by one count") | Same | Never |
| Contested | "Historians disagree," then both sides | Same, and it goes in the What We Can't box | Never |
| Unsupported | Only as Heather's theory, answered by Edwin, or as a question | Only as a labeled rumor | Never |
| Refuted | Only to knock it down | Myth sidebar | Only as the myth being broken |

Floors:

- A sentence that states a fact as fact in the magazine body needs Likely or above. Contested, Unsupported and Refuted material appears only in the forms the table allows.
- The cold open, the craziest part and the withheld object need Proven, or Strongly supported (proposed default, not yet approved).
- A hedge belongs in the same sentence as the fact. A hedge two sentences later does not count, because clips and pull quotes drop it.
- Living people have extra limits. An Unsupported claim about a living person is never aired as a theory. See page 05 (Never-Break Rules and Legal).

## On screen and on the page

**No stamps or badges on screen.** Proof levels never appear on screen as stamps, badges or labels (DEC-2026-017, DEC-2026-002). The words carry the level.

**The opinion corner tag.** Opinion lines are said strongly. The screen shows "opinion" in the corner while the line plays. Scripts and outlines carry no opinion tags. Each piece keeps a separate Opinion Register (line, speaker, the facts it rests on, living person or not), described in page 05.

**What We Can Prove / What We Can't.** Every magazine feature ends with this box.

- What We Can Prove: the Proven and Strongly supported points behind the thesis.
- What We Can't: every Contested point, open UNREAD items that matter to the story, and popular claims we found Unsupported.

The back-of-issue sources section matches the fact-check log one to one. Each feature's sources doc shows the full six-level rating for every claim.

## Mapping old labels to the six levels

Older docs, scripts and skills use at least five other scales. Convert every old label before you use the row. When a mapping says "re-rate," go back to the sources and apply the definitions above. Never convert a whole file at once by find-and-replace.

| Old label | Where it was used | New level |
|---|---|---|
| Documented (primary seen, or several chains) | MASTER per-fact scale, sources docs | Proven |
| Documented (secondary, one chain) | MASTER, sources docs, dossiers | Strongly supported |
| DOCUMENTED* | Older Shorts check files | Strongly supported |
| Strongly supported (old usage) | MASTER per-claim scale, Story Templates, Vol. 1 sources doc | Strongly supported only if it meets the new definition (one sound scholarly chain). Otherwise re-rate. |
| Likely | MASTER, Story Templates | Likely (confirm nothing stands against it) |
| Plausible | MASTER per-claim scale | Likely |
| REPORTED, Reported | Older Shorts check files, desk notes | Likely. If the report is about a living person, apply the split rule: "the outlet reported X" is rated on one line, X on its own line. |
| Contested, Disputed | MASTER, dossiers | Contested |
| Weak (with some evidence) | MASTER per-claim scale | Contested |
| Weak (no evidence) | MASTER per-claim scale | Unsupported |
| Unsupported, Attributed | MASTER, dossiers | Unsupported |
| Refuted | MASTER | Refuted |
| Not proven | Vol. 1 sources doc, print proof boxes | Contested, Unsupported or Refuted. Split it row by row. |
| PROVEN (Pipeline 09) | Episode Pipeline v2 | Proven or Strongly supported. Re-rate each row. |
| LIKELY, CONTESTED, UNSUPPORTED (Pipeline 09) | Episode Pipeline v2 | Likely, Contested, Unsupported (confirm each against the definitions) |
| PROVEN / LIKELY / NOT PROVEN stamps | Story Templates badge column, older scripts | Retired. Re-rate from the row underneath. Never show on screen. |
| On-screen badges of any kind | Story Templates, older scripts | Retired. No replacement on screen. |
| "[D, allegation]" | Older living-person check files | Split rule: "a filing alleges X" is rated on one line; X is rated on its own line. Use court and filing language (page 05). |

If you meet a label that is not in this table, rate the row from its sources and tell the Research Librarian so the label can be added.

## Worked examples of safe wording

These examples use an invented case: a 1921 city council, a mayor, and a story that the mayor took a bribe to sell the waterworks. They show wording only.

| Level | The fact | Video wording | Magazine wording | Packaging |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proven | The council minutes record the vote as 7 to 2 (minutes seen) | "The council voted 7 to 2." | "The council voted 7 to 2 to sell the waterworks." | "The 7-to-2 Vote" is allowed |
| Strongly supported | A university history cites a letter, not seen by us, in which the mayor asked for the sale | "According to the city's official history, the mayor asked for the sale himself." | "The mayor asked for the sale himself, according to the city's 1988 official history." | Allowed, with the source named in the body (proposed default, not yet approved) |
| Likely | One newspaper says 400 people protested | "One paper counted about 400 protesters." | "About 400 people protested, by one paper's count." | Never. Write the title from a Proven or Strongly supported fact. |
| Contested | Two historians disagree on who wrote the sale terms | "Historians disagree. One says the mayor wrote the terms. Another says the buyer's lawyer did." | Same, plus a line in the What We Can't box | Never |
| Unsupported | The popular story that the mayor took a bribe; no evidence found | Heather: "My theory? Somebody got paid." Edwin answers with what the record shows. Or as a question: "So did anyone get paid? Nothing in the record says so." | "A rumor, repeated for a century, says the mayor was paid. We found no record of it." | Never |
| Refuted | The story that the sale happened in secret; the minutes show a public meeting | "The famous version says it happened in secret. The minutes say it was a public meeting with a reporter in the room." | Myth sidebar: "Myth: the sale was secret. Record: a public meeting." | Only as the myth being broken: "The Secret Sale That Wasn't" |

Split rule example, same case:

| Line | Level |
|---|---|
| The 1950 memoir quotes the mayor saying, "The city can't afford water." | Proven (we read the memoir) |
| The mayor said, "The city can't afford water." | Likely (one late chain, nothing against it) |

Video wording: "In a memoir written 30 years later, an aide remembers the mayor saying the city couldn't afford water." The attribution is said flat. The quote is hedged in the same sentence.

UNREAD example: the city's 1921 newspaper archive is blocked. The vote count stays at its last level, the line runs as "The council voted, by the minutes' count, 7 to 2," and the archive goes on the Source Access Registry with the exact search that would settle it.

# 03 · Sources and Quotes
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page says what counts as a source, how to count independent evidence, how to check and print a quote, and how to write a citation in the sources doc. Use it every time you add a row to a Fact Ledger, a sources doc or a fact-check log.

## The short version

- Every fact traces to a tier 1 to 4 source. Tier 5 points the way. Tier 6 is never a source.
- Count chains, not citations. Write the chain in the source column.
- Every quote carries a quote level (Q0 to Q3). Only Q1 and Q2 run as plain quotes.
- "First", "only", "never" and "nobody" need a logged search scope.
- Anything you read through a summarizing tool is checked by eye before print.
- Every source row records how you reached it.

## Source tiers

| Tier | Source type | Examples | Use rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Primary: the document itself | Archive image, Hansard, court docket, patent, diary, will, a period newspaper page | Any use. Can support Proven. |
| 2 | Published edition or transcription of a primary | A library's typed transcript, a foundation's published text of a will | Any use. Note the edition. Crowd transcriptions are provisional: check the page image before print. |
| 3 | Scholarly secondary | Peer-reviewed article, academic press book, national biography dictionary, official history | Any use. Record the author's own sources. |
| 4 | Press, reference and popular history | Magazines, museum sites, encyclopedias, popular books and videos | Supporting only. Never the top of a Proven or Strongly supported claim. |
| 5 | Wikis and aggregators | Wikipedia, fan sites, list sites, search snippets | Pointers only. Follow the footnote and cite what it cites. Never the sole support for a claim. |
| 6 | AI output and our own work | Any AI answer or AI search summary, our agents' reports, our scripts, our magazine, memory | Never a source. Leads only. |

### Tier rules to remember

- A period newspaper is tier 1 for what it printed. It is a secondary report of what happened. Rate those two things on separate lines (see page 02, How Sure Are We?, for the split rule).
- Our own research docs are not sources. Cite the source they cite, after you open it.
- "Wikipedia citing X" is X, once you have read X. If you could not read X, the fact is UNREAD at best.
- Watch for wiki-born myths. If the earliest appearance of a "fact" is a recent wiki edit, rate it Unsupported until a tier 1 to 4 source turns up.
- An AI tool can suggest where to look. Before the fact enters the ledger, trace it to tiers 1 to 4 and read that source yourself.

## Count chains, not citations

A chain is the line of sources that all lead back to one original. Ten writers who all quote the same historian are one chain. Name each chain by its earliest source.

How to write a chain in the source column:

1. Start with the earliest source you can identify, with its year.
2. Add "via" and each later source you actually read, in order.
3. Mark anything you did not read yourself as "cited in", not "via".

Examples:

- `Serle 1982 via Coulthart 2014` (one chain: Coulthart repeats Serle).
- `Home Office file [ref] cited in Smith 1998` (one chain, and the file itself is unread).
- `Wikipedia citing Lloyd George papers` becomes `Lloyd George papers [ref]` once you read the papers. If you could not, write `Lloyd George papers, cited in Wikipedia, UNREAD`.

Rules:

- Two chains are independent only if neither one draws on the other and they do not share an earlier source.
- Three readers of one transcript confirm the reading of that transcript. They do not confirm the event. That is one chain.
- When a scholar's own source is a memoir by an interested party, record that. It can drop the rating to Contested (see page 02).
- Proven needs a primary seen or two independent chains that agree. Count chains before you rate.

## Quote levels

| Level | Checked against | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | The primary image or the original-language text | Any use, including hero quotes, on-screen quote cards and the withheld object |
| Q2 | A reliable published transcription or edition | Any use. Note the edition in the citation. |
| Q3 | A secondary that quotes it | Only with the go-between named: "as Serle quotes him" |
| Q0 | Not checked | Paraphrase only. No quote marks. |

A quote bank marked "verified" must say which level. "Checked via a biographer" is Q3, however good the biographer is.

### Exact-quote rules

- Copy the quote character for character, including original spelling and punctuation ("havent" stays "havent").
- Mark every cut with an ellipsis. Never crop a sentence in the middle so it says more than the speaker said.
- Keep a qualifier with its claim. If the speaker hedged ("what you might call"), the hedge stays in.
- Use the fuller form when the lead-in changes the meaning.
- Credit the speaker. If a diarist recorded what someone else said, the speaker is the one who said it, and the diarist is the source.
- On-screen quote cards match the spoken words exactly.
- Fetch tools can return text in pieces of 125 characters or fewer. A quote rebuilt from pieces is flagged "assembled" and rechecked against the source as one piece before it is used.
- Quote only what the source shows. If you can see only a fragment, quote only the fragment.

## Translations

- Say whose English: "our translation", or the translator's or publisher's name.
- Print the original next to the English in the sources doc and, where space allows, in print. On-screen cards say TRANSLATED.
- A second reader checks every translation we make ourselves. Log who.
- When a line survives only in translation, say so and credit the translator.
- Note loaded words and their plain meaning (for example, a French word that can mean "hired killer" as well as "assassin").
- A quote seen only through a translated citation of a book we have not read is Q3.

## Scan page versus printed page

Scanned books often number pages differently from the printed book.

- Cite "scan p." and printed "p." as separate fields. Never mix them.
- Convert to the printed page before print. If you cannot, cite the scan page and the stable ID so a reader can find it.
- Give stable IDs where they exist: newspaper article numbers, archive item IDs, catalogue references.
- Note any OCR fix you made to the text you quote.
- Check the traps: does the weekday match the date? Did the person hold that job title on that date?

## Superlatives and absences need a logged search scope

Words like "first", "only", "earliest", "never", "nobody at the time" are claims about everything that exists. You can only make them about what you searched.

Log the scope in the ledger row and the fact-check log:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Database or archive | A national newspaper database |
| Date range | 1 Oct 1932 to 31 Dec 1932 |
| Search terms | "emu" AND ("war" OR "machine gun") |
| Who ran it, and when | Desk 3, 2026-10-16 |
| What it found | Earliest hit and its ID |

Then match the wording to the scope:

- Regional search only: "the earliest report we found in [region]'s papers". Never "first".
- National search done: "the earliest report in [database]". Use "first" only when the scope covers every record that could hold an earlier case, and say what was searched in the sources doc.
- No search logged: no superlative. Rewrite or cut.

A regional search can make a paper look first when a national search finds an earlier one. Run the wider search before the word goes in.

## Anything read through a summarizing tool is checked by eye

Some tools hand back a summary of a page or a list instead of the text. Counts, names and quotes drift in summaries.

- Mark the ledger row "read via tool" when your only reading came through a summarizer, an AI search answer or a fetch tool that returns a digest.
- Before print or air, open the full text and check the fact by eye. Change the check status to "read by eye" and log who and when.
- If you cannot open the full text, the fact is UNREAD and keeps a guardrail in copy.

Example: a summary says a club had "about 400 members"; the full list, counted by eye, gives a different number. Only the by-eye count may print.

## Record how every source was reached

Every source row says how you got to it. This is how a later reader can repeat your step, and how we show we never went around a block.

| How reached | What to record |
|---|---|
| Direct | URL or catalogue reference, date accessed |
| Alternate legitimate route | Which route (publisher full-text search, library catalogue, in-book search, a second legitimate host of the same public-domain text), URL, date |
| Heather's browser (DEC-2026-003) | Who asked, who opened it, date and time, exactly what was read; matching row in the Source Access Registry |
| Book or copy obtained lawfully | Edition, how obtained (bought, library), who holds it |
| Not reached | Mark UNREAD; add to the Access needed list and the Source Access Registry |

Rules:

- A tool or proxy limit is logged as a tool limit. It is not permission to go around a site.
- A site that refuses you is never bypassed. See page 05 (Never-Break Rules and Legal) for allowed and banned routes and how to ask Heather to open a site.
- If the route is not recorded, the source is treated as not read.

## Citation format for the sources doc

Each claim in a feature's sources doc gets one block, in article or script order. The sources doc rows match the fact-check log one to one.

Fields, in this order:

1. Fact ID (from the Fact Ledger)
2. Claim as printed or spoken
3. Level (one of the six levels; two lines if the split rule applies)
4. Chain (written as above)
5. Citation: author or body, year, title, edition, printed p., scan p., stable ID or URL
6. Tier (1 to 6)
7. Quote level, if the line quotes anyone (Q0 to Q3), and translation note
8. How reached, with date
9. Search scope, if the claim is a superlative or an absence
10. Flags: UNREAD, CHECK, or none

Worked example (shape only; the details are placeholders):

> **F-031** · "The order came from the minister's office."
> Level: Likely
> Chain: Cabinet minutes [ref] cited in Smith 1998 (one chain)
> Citation: Smith, J. (1998). *Title of Book*. 2nd ed. Publisher. p. 112 (scan p. 130). Catalogue ID [ref].
> Tier: 3 (minutes unread)
> Quote level: n/a
> How reached: library e-book, 2026-11-20
> Search scope: n/a
> Flags: UNREAD (minutes); guardrail "according to Smith"

Split-rule example:

> **F-044a** · Attribution: the biographer quotes him saying it. Level: Proven (Q2, edition noted).
> **F-044b** · Content: what he said happened. Level: Likely (one chain).

## Self-check before you hand in

- [ ] Every fact has a tier 1 to 4 source, and no tier 5 or 6 source stands alone.
- [ ] Every chain is written out, and Proven rows show a primary seen or two independent chains.
- [ ] Every quote has a level; Q3 quotes name the go-between; Q0 lines carry no quote marks.
- [ ] Every cut has an ellipsis; no qualifier was dropped; assembled quotes were rechecked as one piece.
- [ ] Every translation says whose English and has a second reader logged.
- [ ] Scan pages and printed pages are separate fields.
- [ ] Every superlative or absence has a logged search scope that matches its wording.
- [ ] Every "read via tool" fact headed for print has been read by eye.
- [ ] Every source row says how it was reached.

# 04 · Checking and Corrections
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page sets out how every fact is checked before it reaches print or air, what "done" means for a deliverable, and how errors are corrected before and after publication. Checkers, research leads and anyone who finds an error use it.

## Independent checker rules

The independent check is gate R7 for the magazine package and part of gate R9 for the YouTube extension (see page 01, The Research Process).

- The checker did no research on the topic and wrote nothing in the piece.
- The lead never closes their own gaps. If the lead checks a fact, that check does not count as independent.
- Writers, art and layout never grade their own work.
- The checker works in a fresh session (for an agent) or from a clean brief (for a person). They read the sources. They do not read the reports' reasoning.
- Outside AI tools (Track B) may help with bounded checks, but never as the last check on anything. Their output goes through a Track H or Track A checker.
- Proposed default, not yet approved: a fresh AI agent in a new session runs all checks; a human checker runs covers and Red-tier items once there is budget (DEC-2026-007).

### What the checker gets

1. A numbered list of the exact lines to check, as they will run.
2. The Fact Ledger and the sources doc.
3. The topic's Must NOT list and Rulings Index entries.
4. Host input (any lines the hosts will say in their own words), filled in before the check starts.
5. The search scope log for any superlative or absence.

If any input is missing, the checker stops and reports it. The check does not start on a guess.

### What the checker returns

One result for every line, in the fact-check log below. "Unchecked" is a correct answer when budget or access ran out. A guess is a failure.

## Budget is reserved first

The checker runs last, so the checker's searches are set aside before anyone else spends.

1. Before R2, count the print-bound facts you expect. Reserve one search per print-bound fact, plus 10, for the checker.
2. Run the check in a fresh session with that reserve.
3. Spend the reserve on the mandatory set first.
4. When the video is confirmed at R9, reserve checking budget the same way for every new air-bound fact, the title and the thumbnail.
5. Any fact still unchecked blocks lock. It is hedged, cut or held. It is never printed or aired at its old rating.

### The mandatory set (funded first)

- [ ] Cover
- [ ] Title
- [ ] Thumbnail
- [ ] Headline
- [ ] Teaser
- [ ] Cold open
- [ ] Craziest part
- [ ] Withheld object
- [ ] Biggest number
- [ ] Twist
- [ ] Other-side quote
- [ ] Every direct quote
- [ ] Every number
- [ ] Every claim about a living person

## Light fact pass at outline stage

Errors caught at outline cost a line. Errors caught in a near-final script cost a rewrite.

When a story outline or spine is ready, someone who did not write it runs a light pass:

1. Give every factual line in the outline a fact ID from the ledger.
2. Mark any line whose wording is stronger than its level (see page 02, How Sure Are We?).
3. Mark any line with no ledger row. It becomes a change request before it can stay.
4. Check the outline against the Must NOT list and the Killed Claims list.
5. Log the result in the research log.

This pass does not replace R7. It catches problems early so R7 has fewer.

## The fact-check log

One log template for every product: magazine, script, Shorts, description, packaging.

### Header

- Product and version
- Checker name (or agent model and version)
- Date
- Scope (which lines, which gate)
- Search budget reserved and used
- Blocked sites met
- Access needed list

### Columns

| Field | What goes in it |

| Safe wording | The wording the level allows |
| Source chain | Tier, citation, printed p. or scan p., ID or URL (see page 03, Sources and Quotes) |

| Search scope | Database, dates, terms (for "first", "only", "never") |
| Result | One result code (below) |

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confirmed | The source says what the line says, at the stated level |
| Confirmed with correction | True once the fix is made |
| Not confirmed | The checker could not find support |

| Severity | Meaning |
|---|---|

A deliverable is verified when all of these hold. The criteria are from plan section 7.4, word for word.

- [ ] every quote is Q1 or Q2, or attributed as Q3

- [ ] open CHECKs are zero or signed off
- [ ] the Must NOT list is checked line by line

## Fix loops

1. The checker returns the log.
2. The writer or lead fixes every MUST FIX and SHOULD FIX row, or logs why a SHOULD FIX stays.
3. The checker re-checks the changed lines only, plus any line the fix touched.
4. At most two fix loops after a failed check. A third failure goes to Heather with both reports, on one decision page.

The lead may rule on wording-level conflicts where the lower rating wins. Anything that changes the thesis, a title word or a locked doc goes to Heather.

# 05 · Never-Break Rules and Legal
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page holds the ten rules every vendor signs and every agent loads, plus the house standards for living people, opinion, images, AI visuals, access to sources, privacy and hate tropes. SIDE EYE has no lawyer, so the legal gate on this page is how we keep risky lines out of print and off air.

## The Never-Break Rules

Every vendor signs these on intake. Every agent loads them in its short system prompt. Some breaks are critical errors that end probation at once: an invented source, a workaround past a block, a hate trope (see page 08, Onboarding).

1. Never invent a quote, fact, source or detail.
2. Never state or imply that a living person committed a crime, fraud, cover-up, sexual misconduct or corrupt deal unless a court, regulator or their own words establish it. Use the exact legal outcome.
3. Never present association as participation (a shared document, club, board or neighbor).
4. Never use antisemitic, Illuminati or similar tropes as evidence or explanation.
5. Never get past a site that refuses access, and never use pirated copies.
6. Never use an image whose licence you did not read yourself.
7. Never generate footage of a real person acting, or anything that passes for a real document.
8. Never name or show a private person who is not central to the story.
9. Never contact a subject, source, archive or lawyer on SIDE EYE's behalf without Heather's yes.
10. Never mark work Approved or Published.

## Living people: risk tiers

## Court and filing vocabulary

Use these words, exactly, when a living person is in a wrongdoing frame. Every statement of guilt tracks the outcome count by count.

| Term | Use it when | Generic example |
|---|---|---|
| charged | A prosecutor filed charges; no verdict yet | "Person X was charged with two counts of..." |
| convicted on counts | A court convicted on some or all counts | "convicted on four counts and acquitted on three" |
| acquitted on counts | A court acquitted on some or all counts | Always say which counts, next to any conviction |
| settled without admission | A case settled and the terms say no liability was admitted | "The company settled without admitting wrongdoing." |
| ruled false | A court ruled a statement false | Say exactly what the court decided, and what it did not decide (for example, falsity decided, liability not decided) |
| alleged in a filing | A claim appears in a court filing and has not been decided | "A 2019 lawsuit alleged that..." |
| reported by an outlet | Only the press has said it | "The [outlet] reported that..." |

Words that need a court outcome before they run about a living person: "fraud", "lied", "cover-up", "bribe", "found liable", "crook", "criminal". A label the person gave themselves, or a claim from a single interested source, carries "he says", "she says" or "they say" every time.

## Proof standards for wrongdoing claims

These apply to the dead as well as the living. Fix them before the red team attacks a claim (gate R5).

- Coordination needs a document that shows agreement.
- Bribery needs money paid before or during the event.
- Being named in a document proves contact, and nothing more.
- Ordinary business (loans, fees, patents, board seats) is legal unless a document says otherwise.
- A network overlap must beat the base rate: how many people in that circle would overlap by chance?
- A suspicious date is a lead. It is not evidence.
- A circular citation counts once (page 03, Sources and Quotes).

## Naming standard

Every research file uses one name pattern:

```
RP-### Topic – Doc type – vX.Y – STATUS – YYYY-MM-DD
```

Example: `RP-004 Keith Murdoch – Sources – v2.0 – APPROVED – 2026-10-16`

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|

| STATUS | DRAFT, IN REVIEW, APPROVED, LOCKED (standards only) or SUPERSEDED. Only Heather sets APPROVED or LOCKED. |
| Date | ISO format in the file name (2026-11-24). Written out in body text (Nov 24, 2026). |

RP-### Topic – Doc type – vX.Y – STATUS – YYYY-MM-DD

# 07 · Role Cards
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page gives one card for each research role: what the role is for, what it owns, what to read, what to return and what it must never do. Read your own card on day one, keep it open while you work, and run its self-check before you hand anything off.

## Before you read your card

Every role, human or agent, follows these rules:

- The Never-Break Rules on page 05 (Never-Break Rules and Legal) apply in full. You sign or load them before any work.
- Use the six levels only: Proven, Strongly supported, Likely, Contested, Unsupported, Refuted. UNREAD and CHECK are flags, not levels.
- Rate attribution and content on separate lines when a source reports a speech or a view.
- Our own reports, scripts, magazine and any AI output are never a source. They can point to a source.
- A blocked site goes on the Access List (page 06, template T03). Never get past it.
- Unchecked is a correct answer. A guess is a failure.
- Nothing counts as delivered until it is filed in Drive under the naming standard on page 06.
- Never mark anything APPROVED, LOCKED or Published. Only Heather does that.
- Agents can't launch agents. The research lead launches every agent directly.

**Asking a question.** Look in the topic's rulings file and Must NOT list, then the Decision Register, then the FAQ on page 12. Still open? Add a row to the open-questions section of the research log with your proposed answer, use the lower level meanwhile, and keep working. The research lead batches questions for Heather at the next gate, and they reach her only through her decisions channel (page 09). Vendors never message Heather directly about research.

## Desk researcher

**Purpose.** Gather what the record says about one slice of the topic, rate every finding, and show exactly where it came from.

**Owns.** One desk at R2 (or one gap team at R3): a set of people, years and questions named in the brief.

**Reads first.**
- The common brief (T01), including established facts, blocked sites and Killed Claims for this topic.
- The current corrected sources doc for the topic.
- The rating scale card on page 02 and the source tiers on page 03.
- This card.

**Returns.** A desk report (T02) with rated findings, a links table, leads not followed and access needed. New access rows also go on the Access List (T03).

**Search cap.** 30. Prefer fetching known pages and full texts over open searches.

**Must never.**
- Rate a historian's claim as if it were the document's. Split attribution from content.
- Count ten writers who quote one source as ten chains.
- Use a wiki or AI answer as the source. Follow the footnote and cite what it cites.
- Leave the report outside Drive.

**Hand-off.** File the report in `01 Desk Reports` with the naming standard. Tell the research lead the file name, searches used and any access rows added.

**Self-check.**
- [ ] Every finding has one of the six levels and at least one link ID.
- [ ] Attribution and content are on separate lines where a source reports a view.
- [ ] Every chain is named by its earliest source ("Author 1982 via Author 2014").
- [ ] Every link row says how the source was reached.
- [ ] "First", "only" and "never" claims carry a search scope (database, dates, terms).
- [ ] Quotes are character-exact, with the qualifier and an ellipsis for each cut.
- [ ] Blocked sources are on the Access List, not worked around.
- [ ] The report is filed in Drive and named correctly.

**Approved example (illustration).**

| # | Finding | Level | Split | Tier | Links |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Biographer B writes that the banker lent money to the general's family in 1799. | Proven | Attribution | 3 | L-07 |
| 5 | The banker lent money to the general's family in 1799. | Likely | Content | 3 | L-07 (Biographer B cites a letter we have not seen) |

Why it passes: the saying and the event are rated on separate lines, and row 5 is not raised above one unseen chain.

**Rejected example (illustration).** "The banker financed the coup. Proven. Source: online encyclopedia." Why it fails: a tier 5 source stands alone, the content is rated as if it were a document, and "financed the coup" goes beyond a loan.

## Book reader

**Purpose.** Read one chunk of one book and report what it says, with page and line for every finding, so nobody has to read it again.

**Owns.** One chunk of a book that Heather has obtained at R3, as set in the reader brief.

**Reads first.**
- The common brief (T01) and the reader brief (chunk, pages, questions).
- The Fact Ledger rows the chunk is meant to test.
- The quote rules on page 03 (Sources and Quotes).

**Returns.** A desk report (T02) for the chunk: page-cited findings, the author's own sources for each, quotes with quote level, and anything that contradicts the ledger.

**Search cap.** 15, mostly to locate the edition or the cited primary. The reading itself needs no searches.

**Must never.**
- Summarize without page and line.
- Mix scan pages with printed pages. Cite "scan p." and printed "p." separately.
- Treat the author's quotation of a primary as a check of the primary. That is Q3.
- Copy more of a copyrighted text than a short quote.

**Hand-off.** File the report in `01 Desk Reports`, named for the book and chunk. Flag any finding that changes a ledger level to the research lead.

**Self-check.**
- [ ] Every finding has a printed page and line (and scan page if different).
- [ ] The author's own source is recorded for each finding.
- [ ] Every quote has a quote level; quotes seen only through the author are marked Q3.
- [ ] Translations say whose English, and the original is kept beside them.
- [ ] Dates checked against weekdays where the source gives both.
- [ ] Contradictions with the ledger are listed by fact ID.

**Approved example (illustration).** "p. 134, lines 12 to 15 (scan p. 211): the author states the dispatch reached the capital in 40 days, citing a named archive file. Level: Strongly supported (one scholarly chain, primary unseen). Quote level for the dispatch wording: Q3, 'as the author quotes it'."

**Rejected example (illustration).** "Chapter 6 makes clear the bankers were involved throughout." Why it fails: no page, no line, no source, and "involved throughout" is the reader's summary dressed as the book's finding.

## Red team

**Purpose.** Try to break one claim before anyone builds on it.

**Owns.** One claim at R5, with its proof standard fixed before the attack started (T06).

**Reads first.**
- The claim and its proof standard (T06).
- The Fact Ledger, the rulings file and the Must NOT list so far.
- The house proof standards on page 06 (T06).
- The red-team prompt (T07).

**Returns.** A red-team report (T07 headings): a verdict on the six levels, chains, what survives as safe wording, Must NOT lines with reasons, corrections and ranked before-print checks. Summary of about 350 words.

**Search cap.** 15.

**Must never.**
- Score the true facts around a claim instead of its central assertion.
- Change the proof standard after the attack begins.
- Treat association (a shared club, board, document or neighbor) as participation.
- Use a trope as evidence or explanation.

**Hand-off.** File the report in `02 Claims and Red Team`. The research lead folds it into the scorecard and Must NOT list (T10).

**Self-check.**
- [ ] The verdict rates the central assertion only.
- [ ] The proof standard used is the one filed before the attack.
- [ ] Independent chains are counted, and circular citations count once.
- [ ] Every Must NOT line has a reason and a safe wording.
- [ ] Corrections and before-print checks are listed and ranked.
- [ ] Blocked sources are on Access needed.

**Approved example (illustration).** Claim: "The three financiers planned the coup together." Proof standard: a document showing agreement. Finding: the three men sat on one board and met in the same month (both Proven), but no letter, minute or account shows agreement. Verdict: Unsupported. Safe wording: "Three of the coup's backers sat on the same board." Must NOT: "They planned it together" (reason: no document shows agreement).

**Rejected example (illustration).** "Five of the six supporting facts are Proven, so the claim is Strongly supported." Why it fails: the surrounding facts were scored instead of the central assertion, and the proof standard (agreement) was never tested.

## Myth-buster

**Purpose.** Make the strongest honest case for the version most people believe, so the thesis has to answer it.

**Owns.** The case against the thesis at R5, and the other-side quote used in print and on video.

**Reads first.**
- The working thesis and supporting points with their levels.
- The Fact Ledger and the scorecard.
- The myth-buster prompt (T08).

**Returns.** A myth-buster report (T08 headings): the case for the myth, the evidence against each point, the best other-side quote with quote level, the thesis's weakest point and safe wording.

**Search cap.** 15, or 30 when the lead runs it as an R6 drill.

**Must never.**
- Soften the counterargument or build a weak version to knock down.
- Rate our own framing. Our framing is never a source.
- Drop a source because it hurts the thesis.

**Hand-off.** File in `02 Claims and Red Team`. The lead uses it for the "other side" in print, the What We Can't box and the video's other-side quote, which the checker then checks.

**Self-check.**
- [ ] The popular version is stated in its strongest sourced form.
- [ ] Every point of the thesis has its best counter-evidence listed.
- [ ] The other-side quote is Q1 or Q2, or marked Q3.
- [ ] The weakest point of the thesis is named plainly.
- [ ] No rating rests on our own work.

**Approved example (illustration).** "The taught version (the general surrendered because the enemy fleet cut off his escape) is supported by the general's own dispatch written after the surrender (tier 1, Q2 via a published edition). Our thesis must explain why that dispatch is wrong or incomplete. Weakest point: our claim about the timing of supplies rests on one chain."

**Rejected example (illustration).** "Some people might still believe the old story, but the evidence is clear." Why it fails: no source, no rating, no other-side quote, and the case for the myth was never made.

## Surprise hunter

**Purpose.** Run down one sharp question in the R6 drill and come back with a clean yes, no or not reached.

**Owns.** One surprise lead from the scorecard or desk reports, with about 30 searches.

**Reads first.**
- The lead it was given, with the fact IDs it would change.
- The Source Access Registry entries for the databases it will need.
- The search-scope rule on page 03.

**Returns.** A short report: the question, the answer (yes, no or not reached), the level, the search scope logged in full, and any new ledger rows proposed. Summary of 200 to 450 words.

**Search cap.** About 30.

**Must never.**
- Turn a "no" into a maybe.
- Claim "first", "only" or "never" from a regional or partial search.
- Get past a blocked database. Log it and say what search would settle it.

**Hand-off.** File in `02 Claims and Red Team` (or `03 Checks` if the lead asks). The lead adds any new rows to the ledger. Anything found after R8 goes in as a change request (T20).

**Self-check.**
- [ ] The answer is yes, no or not reached, in one line.
- [ ] Search scope logged: database, dates, terms.
- [ ] Any "first", "only" or "never" covers the full scope it claims.
- [ ] Blocked databases are on the Access List with the exact search to run.
- [ ] Proposed ledger rows have a level and a chain.

**Approved example (illustration).** "Question: did any paper print the story before the city daily did? Searched: national newspaper database, 1 Oct to 30 Nov of that year, terms [list]. Answer: yes. A regional paper ran it one day earlier. Level: Proven (page image seen, Q1). Effect: the city daily becomes 'the paper that carried it east', and 'first' is removed."

**Rejected example (illustration).** "Couldn't find anything earlier, so it's Likely the city daily was first." Why it fails: an absence turned into a positive claim with no logged scope, and the search did not cover the whole country.

## Independent checker

**Purpose.** Check every line that will be printed or aired against its sources, independently of the people who researched and wrote it.

**Owns.** The numbered list of print- and air-bound lines at R7, the video lines, title and thumbnail at R9, and every change request (T20). Writes the Rating changes rows in the Fact Ledger.

**Reads first.**
- The numbered list, the Fact Ledger and the sources doc.
- The rulings file and the Must NOT list.
- The checker prompt (T09) and the fact-check log (T12).
- Page 04 (Checking and Corrections).

**Returns.** A fact-check log (T12) with a cover sheet, one result per line: Confirmed, Confirmed with correction, Not confirmed, Wrong or Unchecked. Each fix with severity and route code.

**Search cap.** One search per print-bound fact plus 10, set aside before R2 and run in a fresh session. The mandatory set is funded first. Working default: a fresh AI agent in a new session for all checks, and a human for covers and Red-tier items once there is budget (proposed default, not yet approved).

**Must never.**
- Check anything they researched or wrote.
- Mark a fact Confirmed from our own drafts, reports or the research reasoning.
- Leave a result blank. Unchecked is the answer when budget or access runs out.
- Let a mandatory-set fact run unchecked at its old level. It is hedged, cut or held.

**Hand-off.** File the log in `03 Checks`. Send the lead the cover sheet counts. At most two fix loops follow a failed check; a third failure goes to Heather with both reports. During vendor probation the checker reviews 100% of a new vendor's rows on job 1, 25% on job 2 and 10% after, and every print-bound fact every time.

**Self-check.**
- [ ] I did no research on this topic and wrote nothing in this piece.
- [ ] The mandatory set was checked first.
- [ ] Every quote was checked character for character, as one piece, with its qualifier.
- [ ] Every "first", "only", "never" has a search scope I could confirm.
- [ ] Anything read through a summarizing tool was checked by eye.
- [ ] Every line has a result; none is blank.
- [ ] Every level that moved has a Rating changes row.
- [ ] The cover sheet counts are filled in.

**Approved example (illustration).** "Line 14, quote card. Checked against the page image (Q1). The draft dropped the speaker's opening qualifier, which changes the meaning. Result: Confirmed with correction. Fix: restore the full sentence with the qualifier; ellipsis for the cut at the end. Severity: MUST. Route: FACT."

**Rejected example (illustration).** "Lines 22 to 30: budget ran out, but these match the sources doc, so Confirmed." Why it fails: the sources doc is our own work, the lines were never checked, and the honest result is Unchecked.

## Network keeper

**Purpose.** Keep the people-and-links data for a topic sourced, rated and reusable across packages.

**Owns.** The topic's nodes and edges sheets in `05 Data`, and its rows in the master Network Database in `03 Reference Library`.

**Reads first.**
- The Fact Ledger and the desk reports.
- The column rules for data sheets: every row carries the RP-ID, a level, a source ID and a row status (Researched, SEED, Refuted).
- The proof standard for network overlap (T06): overlap must beat the base rate.

**Returns.** Updated nodes and edges sheets, filed in Drive, with a short note of rows added, changed or refuted.

**Search cap.** 15.

**Must never.**
- Add a link without a level and a source.
- Label a shared club, board or document as a conspiracy, plot or participation.
- Mix SEED rows with researched rows. SEED rows live on their own tab.
- Keep the only copy outside Drive.

**Hand-off.** File in `05 Data`, append to the master sheets keyed by RP-ID, and tell the research lead what changed. CSV copies for agents are built from the sheets, never the other way round.

**Self-check.**
- [ ] Every edge has a relation type, dates, a level and a source ID.
- [ ] Relation types describe what the source shows (co-director, lender, correspondent).
- [ ] SEED rows are on their own tab.
- [ ] Living people's rows carry their risk tier.
- [ ] The sheets are in Drive and the master sheets are updated.

**Approved example (illustration).** `RP-### · Person A → Person B · relation: co-director, Company C · 1921 to 1925 · level: Proven · source S-044 · status: Researched`

**Rejected example (illustration).** `Person A → Person B · "conspired" · source: forum thread`. Why it fails: no level, a tier 5 source, and a relation type that claims more than any document shows.

## Research lead

**Purpose.** Run one research package from intake to hand-off, keep its record, and bring Heather only the questions only she can answer.

**Owns.** One package from R0 to R8 (and R9 for video): the common brief, the research log, the Fact Ledger, the conflict register, the scorecard and Must NOT list, the rulings file, the decision-needed pages and the hand-off. Launches every agent directly. Holds the package budget, including the checker reserve.

**Reads first.**
- The Decision Register, the Killed Claims list and the Source Access Registry.
- Existing Drive research and sources docs on the topic (R1 inventory).
- Pages 01 (The Research Process) to 05, and every role card.

**Returns.** The package, gate by gate: the files listed in the magazine hand-off checklist (T21) at R8, and the video checklist at R9.

**Search cap.** Holds the budget: about 200 searches for Phase 1, 30 per desk agent, 15 for red team and quick checks, and the checker reserve set aside before R2. Uses few searches of its own.

**Must never.**
- Close their own gaps or check their own work.
- Rule on a conflict that changes the thesis, a title word or a locked doc. Those go to Heather on a decision-needed page.
- Skip a gate without a waiver row in the research log.
- Ask Heather something the Decision Register already answers.
- Let a script, title or layout start before the package passes its gate.

**Hand-off.** At R8, the magazine hand-off checklist (T21) with Heather's acceptance logged. At R9, the video checklist. At the end of every session, a handoff note in the research log.

**Self-check.**
- [ ] The question is testable, with names, places and years, and Heather approved it.
- [ ] Tier, risk tier, budget and checker reserve were set at R0.
- [ ] Every agent and vendor has a role card and a scoped brief.
- [ ] Every conflict is logged; the lower level is used until a ruling closes it.
- [ ] Only wording-level conflicts were ruled alone; the rest went to Heather.
- [ ] Every skipped step has a waiver row.
- [ ] Questions for Heather are batched on one decision-needed page per gate, each with 2 to 4 options, a pick, examples, a deadline and a priority, and sent through her decisions channel.
- [ ] The research log has a handoff note for this session.

**Approved example (illustration).** Two files give different counts for a list of members. The lead logs a conflict row, uses the lower count in all drafts with "by one count", and records a wording-level ruling. A second conflict would change a thesis point, so it goes on the gate's decision-needed page with two options side by side and a recommendation.

**Rejected example (illustration).** The lead finds three checker items left Unchecked, checks them alone, marks them Confirmed and passes R7. Why it fails: the lead researched the topic, so the check is not independent; the items should be hedged, cut or held, or sent to a fresh checker.

## Research librarian

**Purpose.** Keep the shared records clean and findable, release portal versions, and catch strays before they cause a miss.

**Owns.** The Decision Register, the Research Register, the Killed Claims list, the Rulings Index, the Source Access Registry, the Incident Log and the Change Log, all in `02 Logs`. Portal releases. The monthly audit. This is a standing role, filled by a Claude thread or a vendor.

**Reads first.**

- The release steps on page 13 (Change Log).

**Returns.**

- A monthly audit note listing strays, duplicates and wrong statuses, with fixes.
- An Incident Log review after each issue ships.

**Search cap.** No topic research. Drive searches stay inside the Research folder.

**Must never.**
- Set APPROVED or LOCKED, or release a page Heather has not approved.
- Trash a file, or leave a superseded file beside the live one.
- Hand-edit a generated Markdown or CSV copy. Edit the master and rebuild.
- Edit a package's Fact Ledger or ratings.
- Move existing research into package folders without Heather's yes. Working default: index it where it is from the Research Register (proposed default, not yet approved).

**Hand-off.** Each release ends with matching "vX.Y · date" lines on the Drive doc, the web page and the AI files. Each audit ends with a note in `02 Logs`.

**Self-check.**
- [ ] Every new decision has a DEC ID, a link to where it was made, and a status.
- [ ] Every package in the Research Register has one live file per deliverable.
- [ ] No folder carries "CURRENT"; no superseded file sits beside a live one.
- [ ] Title and first line match on every file checked.
- [ ] Versions match across Drive, web and AI files.
- [ ] Vendor sharing is at project-folder level only, with "editors can share" off.
- [ ] Offboarded vendors had sharing removed within one working day.

**Approved example (illustration).** A sources doc moves from v1.1 to v2.0 after a rating change. The librarian renames the old file `SUPERSEDED (use v2.0) – RP-### Topic – Sources – v1.1 – APPROVED – [date]`, moves it to `_Superseded` the same day, and updates the Research Register row.

**Rejected example (illustration).** A folder named "Sources (CURRENT)" holds "Sources v2 corrected" beside "Sources v1", and an older draft numbered v5 sits next to a newer v1. Why it fails: no reader can tell which file is live, and version numbers restarted.

# 08 · Onboarding Vendors and Agents
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page is how a new human vendor or AI agent gets ready to research for SIDE EYE: which track you are on, what to read, the test you pass, the access you get, and how you leave. Nobody works on a live topic until they have passed certification.

## The three tracks

Everyone learns the same core: the one rule ("Test the conspiracy. Don't assume it."), the six-level scale and the Never-Break Rules. What you may do depends on your track.

| Track | Who | Can do | Can't do |
|---|---|---|---|
| H: Human vendor | Freelance researcher, fact-checker, editor | The roles you are certified for | Rule on conflicts; mark anything APPROVED or LOCKED |
| A: Connected agent | Claude with Drive tools, scoped to named folders | Any role, launched directly by a research lead | Search or read outside its named folders |
| B: Outside AI | Other AI tools working from a pasted pack | Bounded tasks: read one text, check a numbered list | Write to Drive; be the last check on anything |

Rules for all tracks:

- Track B output always goes through an independent checker on Track H or Track A before anyone uses it.
- Certified Track H and Track A may do original research. Track B only reads and checks. (Proposed default DEC-2026-008, not yet approved.)
- Agents can't launch agents. The research lead launches every agent directly.
- Nobody on any track marks work APPROVED, LOCKED or Published. Only Heather does.

## Day-one reading list (about 3 hours)

Read in this order. Tick each item in your onboarding log.

- [ ] Page 00 (Start Here): the one rule, who decides what, where to go by role (10 min)
- [ ] Page 02 (How Sure Are We?): the six-level scale card and the map from old labels (10 min)
- [ ] Page 01 (The Research Process): gates R0 to R9, plus the MASTER Deep Research Process Steps 1 to 9 and its traps table (40 min)
- [ ] Template A, sections A1 to A22, from page 06 (Templates) (40 min)
- [ ] Your role card from page 07 (Role Cards) (10 min)
- [ ] The Rulings Index on page 09 (Decisions and Rulings) and two worked Must NOT lists from the starter kit (30 min)
- [ ] One finished fact-check log as a model (the Perot EP03 log, section 1) (20 min)
- [ ] Page 05 (Never-Break Rules and Legal), then sign it (10 min)
- [ ] "How to ask a question" at the end of this page (5 min)

AI agents get page 00, the scale card, their role card and the Never-Break Rules inside the short system prompt. The rest comes as reference files.

## Starter kit

The starter kit lives in Research > Standards and Processes > 05 Onboarding. Copy each blank; never edit the master.

- The file-naming rule (page 10, Research Register)

## Certification (about 2.5 hours)

The test is the same for people and agents. It uses real cases from past SIDE EYE work. You take it once before your first job, with one retake after feedback.

### Module 1: Must NOT spotter

You get twelve draft lines from a past feature. Mark each one OK or FIX. For every FIX, name the rule it breaks and write safe wording. Some lines are already sound. Changing a sound line costs a point.

Pass: at least 10 of 12 correct.

### Module 2: Independent re-check

You get ten print-bound facts and the sources file for that feature. Report each fact as Confirmed, Confirmed with correction, Not confirmed or Wrong, with the exact wording that runs. Some facts contain seeded errors.

Pass: every seeded error caught, and no wrong fact marked Confirmed. Marking a wrong fact Confirmed is an automatic fail.

### Module 3: Divergence rows and a ruling

You fill Template A divergence rows for four "taught version" lines from a past episode, then write a ruling note on two conflicts between sources. Watch for date traps, such as a weekday that doesn't match the date.

Pass: ratings within one step of the key on at least 80% of rows, and no rating raised above its evidence.

### Rules for the whole test

- Any invented quote or source fails the whole test.
- "Unchecked" is a correct answer when you could not check something. A guess is a failure.
- Answer keys stay in a folder only Heather and the research lead can see.
- Case sets rotate between past topics, because a public AI may remember a key it has seen.
- Heather signs certifications during the pilot, then a named research lead. (Proposed default DEC-2026-009, not yet approved.)

## Role assignment

The research lead assigns roles after certification. A role is one of the cards on page 07: desk researcher, book reader, red team, myth-buster, surprise hunter, independent checker, network keeper, research lead, research librarian.

- You work only in roles you are certified for.
- The independent checker did no research on the topic and wrote nothing in the piece.
- The research log roster records your role, whether you are human or agent, the vendor or model and version, and your dates.

## Access rules

- Use your own Google account. No shared passwords, because a shared password can't be removed for one person. (Proposed default DEC-2026-010, not yet approved.)
- You are shared at the project folder only: Research > the issue or product folder > your package. Never the Side Eye root, a parent folder or My Drive.
- "Editors can share" is turned off on every shared research folder.
- Standards and Processes: view only for certified vendors. Training cases: view only. Answer keys: Heather and the research lead only.
- You get one drop folder for drafts.
- Connected agents run with a brief that names their folder. Every search is limited to that folder, and the agent reads only the files its brief names.
- Out of reach for everyone: personal files, mail, calendar, and any host's last name, phone, email or location.

## Probation and sampling

Probation is your first three jobs or 30 days, whichever is longer.

| Job | Rows the checker samples |
|---|---|
| Job 1 | 100% |
| Job 2 | 25% |
| Job 3 and after | 10% |

Every print-bound or air-bound fact is checked every time, whatever the sample rate.

| Error class | Examples | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Invented quote or source, a raised rating, a workaround past a blocked site, a hate trope | One ends probation |
| Major | Wrong date, name or title; a dropped hedge; one source chain counted twice | 2 per 50 rows |
| Minor | Format | Fix and move on |

AI agents are certified per model and version. When the model or version changes, the agent re-runs Module 2 before live work.

## Offboarding (within one working day)

- [ ] Remove all sharing for the vendor or agent
- [ ] Move ownership of every file they created to the project
- [ ] Collect open leads and access-needed items into a handover note in the research log
- [ ] Close their drop folder
- [ ] Delete any custom GPT, Gem or project that holds the research guide
- [ ] Change any shared password that was used
- [ ] Log the exit date in the research log roster

## How to ask a question

Search before you ask. Most questions are already answered.

1. Look in the topic's rulings file and Must NOT list.
2. Look in the Rulings Index and the Decision Register on page 09 (Decisions and Rulings).
3. Look in the FAQ on page 12 (Glossary and FAQ).
4. Still open? Add a row to the topic's question file with: the question, where you looked, your proposed answer, the options, and what each option changes.
5. Use the lower rating meanwhile and keep working.
6. The research lead batches open rows into one decision page at Heather's next gate. Each answer goes into the rulings file and the Rulings Index the same day. If it changes a role card or agent file, that file is updated too.

Vendors never message Heather directly about research.

## What the Research Register is

The Research Register is one Google Sheet in Research > 02 Logs. It has one row per research package. If a package is not in the Register, it does not exist as far as the system is concerned.

### Fields

| Field | What goes in it |

## File naming

Every file name follows one pattern:

**RP-### Topic – Doc type – vX.Y – STATUS – YYYY-MM-DD**

| Version | When |

# 11 · Access and Blocked Sources
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page tells you what to do when a source refuses you: what counts as a block, which other routes are allowed, how to ask Heather to open a site in her own browser (DEC-2026-003), and what gets logged. The short version: never get past a block yourself, log it, keep working, and ask through the registry.

## What counts as a block

A block is any time a source refuses to show you its content. Log every one, even if you found the text another way.

| Block type | What you see |
|---|---|
| Bot challenge | A "checking your browser" page, a puzzle, a box to tick, or a wall that says you look automated |
| Paywall | The page asks you to subscribe or pay to read |
| Login | The page asks you to sign in or use a library card |
| Geo-block | The page says it isn't available in your country or region |
| Rate limit | An error such as 429, "too many requests", or the site works and then stops |
| Robots refusal | The site's robots rules or its API terms bar automated tools |
| Refused or error | A 403, a timeout or an empty page, with no reason given |
| Tool limit | Your own research tool or its proxy refuses the site, while the site itself may be open |

A tool limit is logged as a tool limit. It is never permission to go around the site.

## Allowed and not-allowed routes

Every source in the Fact Ledger records how it was reached: direct, another allowed route (name it), or Heather's browser (with who opened it and when).

### Allowed

- Open-access routes the site itself offers
- A publisher's own full-text or in-book search, and snippet search where it loads (cite the page)
- Library catalogues
- A second legitimate host of the same public-domain text (another national library, a university repository, an official edition)
- Books obtained lawfully (bought, borrowed, or from a library)
- An author's own copy or an open thesis, rated as what it is
- Heather opening the site in her own browser, logged as below

### Not allowed

- Solving or getting past a bot challenge yourself
- Mirrors, caches or archive copies of a page that refused you
- Shadow libraries or pirated copies
- Borrowed or shared logins
- Paywall workarounds of any kind
- Any trick that makes your tool look like a person

Using any not-allowed route is a Critical error (page 08, Onboarding Vendors and Agents). It ends probation.

## Common blocks to expect

Some kinds of source block automated tools often. Plan for them at R1 and put them in the common brief's blocked-site list.

- National newspaper archives often block automated tools with bot challenges, and many bar their APIs.
- National library scan sites and scholarly journal platforms often refuse automated reads.
- Scanned-book sites and full-text libraries rate-limit, or are refused by research tool proxies.
- Video watch pages often refuse transcript reads.
- Government archive and records sites often refuse or time out.
- Nonprofit filing lookups and web archives are sometimes refused.

The full list of sources seen blocked so far, with their routes, is in the Source Access Registry in Research > 02 Logs. Check it before you start a desk.

## Asking Heather to open a site (DEC-2026-003)

Agents may ask Heather to open a blocked site in her own browser. Agents never get past a block themselves.

### Step by step

1. **Check the registry.** If the source already has an approved route other than Heather's browser, use that route.
2. **Try the allowed routes.** Try at least one other allowed route from the list above. Log what you tried.
3. **Add or update the registry row.** Fill in source, what it holds, block type, date seen, the package, and what you will do meanwhile.
4. **Write the request.** Give the exact item: full citation, the search terms or page you need, and the fact IDs it would settle. Say what changes if it is found and if it isn't.
5. **Wait for the gate.** If the work can wait, the request waits for the next Heather gate. The research lead batches all requests for that gate into one list. Ask between gates only when a gate can't pass without the source.
6. **Heather opens it.** She opens the site in her own browser and reads the items the request names, or shares them with the lead.
7. **Log the result.** The lead fills in who opened it, when, and exactly what was read.
8. **Update the ledger.** New findings get Fact Ledger rows and are checked independently like any other fact. Clear the UNREAD flag only for the items actually read.

### What gets logged

The Source Access Registry has one row per source, with one entry per request.

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Source | Site or archive name |
| What it holds | Collection and date range |
| Block type seen | From the table above |
| First seen | First date blocked |
| Seen in | Package IDs |
| What happened | What was and was not read |
| Approved route | The route to use next time |
| Owner | Who is responsible for getting access |
| Status | Open, Partial or Resolved |
| Meanwhile | The guardrail and the exact search that would settle it |
| Browser request | Who asked, at which gate, on what date |
| Opened by, date | Who opened it and when |
| What was read | Exact items with stable IDs (article IDs, page numbers, searches run), and the fact IDs updated or "nothing found" with the search scope |

The row format is template T24 on page 06 (Templates).

## While a source is blocked

### The UNREAD flag

A fact whose source is cited but blocked gets the UNREAD flag in the Fact Ledger. UNREAD is a flag, never a level.

- Keep the fact at its last rating. Never raise it, and never use the block as a reason to drop it to Unsupported.
- Add a guardrail to any copy that uses it.
- Write down the exact search that would settle it (archive, dates, terms).
- An UNREAD fact in the mandatory set (title, thumbnail, cold open, withheld object and the rest of the list on page 04, Checking and Corrections) is hedged, cut or held at R7.

### Guardrail wording

A guardrail tells the reader what we could and couldn't read. Write it plainly, in the same sentence or the next.

- "A 1982 biography says the story ran in dozens of papers. We couldn't read the papers themselves."
- "By one historian's count, the order went out on Nov 2. The original order wasn't reachable."
- "Reports at the time are said to name him. We haven't seen those reports."

Never write a guardrail that hints the answer is hidden or suppressed. A blocked site is a blocked site.

### Keep working

A block stops one fact. The rest of the package keeps moving.

- [ ] Log the block and file the request
- [ ] Flag the fact UNREAD with its last rating
- [ ] Add the guardrail to every draft that uses it
- [ ] Move on to the next fact or desk
- [ ] List the item in your report's access-needed section with a full citation

The checker treats UNREAD facts as unchecked. At R7 each one is hedged, cut or held. It is never printed or aired at a rating it has not earned.

# 12 · Glossary and FAQ
*v0.1 · DRAFT · 2026-11-24 · Owner: Research Librarian · Approved by: not yet approved*

This page defines the words SIDE EYE research uses and answers the questions new vendors and agents ask most. If a term here disagrees with an older doc or skill, this page wins (page 13, Change Log, explains precedence).

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Desk | One research lane at R2 (Gather), covering one set of people, years and questions. A desk is run by a desk researcher, human or agent. |
| Desk report | The standard report a desk returns: summary, rated findings, links table, leads, and an access-needed list. It is filed in the package's 01 Desk Reports folder before the gate closes. |
| Red team | At R5, one agent per claim whose job is to attack that claim. It scores the central claim, never the true facts around it. |
| Myth-buster | At R5, the person or agent who builds the strongest case against the thesis. They never soften the counterargument. |
| Surprise hunter | At R6, a specialist given one sharp question and about 30 searches to chase a surprise lead. They never turn a "no" into a "maybe". |
| Drill | The R6 surprise drill: surprise hunters chase the best leads before the thesis is final. It is run, or waived with a line in the research log. |
| Must NOT list | A numbered list, per topic, of lines that must never be said, each with its reason. Every downstream brief loads it, and lint checks drafts against it. |
| Safe wording | The exact words allowed for a fact at its current level. Writers copy it; they don't improve it. |
| Scorecard | The R5 record of each claim: its level, the evidence for and against, the Must NOT list, corrections, and before-print checks. |
| Fact Ledger | One sheet per package holding every fact: fact ID, statement with its hedge, level, source chain, check status, last changed, and where it is used. Every format reads from it. It is the master when products disagree. |
| Fact ID | The stable ID of one Fact Ledger row, such as F-012. Every factual sentence in a deliverable carries one. |
| Chain | A source chain, named by its earliest source: "Serle 1982 via Coulthart 2014". Count chains when you weigh evidence. Ten writers quoting one source are one chain. |
| Q-levels | How a quote was checked. Q1: against the primary or original-language text. Q2: against a reliable published transcription. Q3: only as quoted by a secondary ("as Serle quotes him"). Q0: unchecked, so paraphrase only. |
| UNREAD | A flag (never a level) on a fact whose source is cited but blocked. Keep the last rating and add a guardrail. See page 11. |
| CHECK | A flag (never a level) marking an open item. Open CHECKs must be zero, or each signed off, before a gate passes. |
| Tier A, B, C | Research tiers. Tier A (lead features, Case Files, covers) runs every gate. Tier B (sidebars, Shorts cut from approved work) runs R1, R4 and R7 against an existing ledger. Tier C (opinion pieces, host banter) runs R7 on any fact it states. Source tiers are numbered 1 to 6 and are a different thing (page 03). |
| Gate | A checkpoint, R0 to R9, with entry and exit criteria. Work moves on only when the gate passes or is waived. R0 to R8 build the magazine package; R9 is the YouTube extension. |
| Waiver | A line in the research log that lets a gate be skipped: the gate, the reason, and who approved it. |
| Change request | How a new fact enters after R8 (or during R9): it gets a ledger row, a rating and an independent check before anyone uses it. |
| Template A | The video research dossier, sections A1 to A22. At R9 it is filled from the same Fact Ledger as the magazine package. |
| SEED | A placeholder row that has not been researched ("SEED, verify"). SEED rows stay out of approved data and are never used in copy. |
| Cold open | The opening scene of a video, before the title. Its facts are in the mandatory set and are checked first. |
| Withheld object | The document or object a video holds back and reveals late. It is in the mandatory set, and any quote on it must be Q1 or Q2. |
| Rulings Index | One list of every ruling across all topics: a disputed count, quote, date or name, and what was decided. See page 09. |
| Decision Register | The sheet of every decision Heather has made about research, with IDs (DEC-YYYY-NNN). Search it before asking her anything. See page 09. |
| Killed Claims | The list of every claim ruled out across all topics, with the reason and safe wording. Lint checks drafts against it. See page 09. |
| Legal gate | A checklist run by someone who did not write the piece. There is no lawyer (DEC-2026-005). Any line that puts a living person in a wrongdoing frame is softened to court and filing language or cut. |
| Red / Amber / Green | The living-person risk tier, set at R0. Green: dead or historical. Amber: a living public figure, documented facts only. Red: a living person plus crime, fraud, sex, health, coordination or money-for-favors, or any private person. |
| Propagation | What happens after any correction: the Fact Ledger, the sources doc, every product that used the fact (magazine, script, Shorts, description) and any template or skill example that teaches the error are all updated. |

## FAQ

### Which version is current?

Look up the package in the Research Register (page 10). Its row gives the live version and a link. In the folder, the live file is the one without a SUPERSEDED prefix, and its name and first line show the same version. Retired versions sit in _Superseded. If you find two live files for one deliverable, use neither until the librarian fixes it, and log it.

### A site blocked me. What do I do?

Never get past it yourself. Log it in the Source Access Registry, try an allowed route, flag the fact UNREAD at its last rating, add a guardrail, and keep working. If no allowed route works, file a request for Heather to open it in her own browser; it waits for the next gate unless the gate can't pass without it. Full steps are on page 11 (Access and Blocked Sources).

### Two files disagree. Which do I use?

The lower rating wins until a ruling is logged. Flag the item as disputed in the Fact Ledger, check the Rulings Index for an existing ruling, and if there is none, add it to the question file. Don't "fix" either file to match the other.

### I found a new fact after R8. Can I use it?

Not yet. Raise a change request: the fact gets a ledger row, a rating and an independent check. It may be used only after the check returns a result. This applies to writers, art and layout too.

### Can I say Strongly supported flat?

Yes, with the source named. On video, say it flat and name the source. In print, the same. On titles and thumbnails it is allowed, with the source named in the body. (Proposed default DEC-2026-006, not yet approved.) Likely must always be hedged in the same sentence, and it never goes in a title or thumbnail.

### Can an AI be the checker?

Yes, if it is a fresh agent in a new session that did no research on the topic and wrote nothing in the piece. A human checker is added for covers and Red-tier items once there is budget. (Proposed default DEC-2026-007, not yet approved.) An outside AI (Track B) is never the last check on anything.

### Who do I ask?

Nobody, until you have searched: the topic's rulings and Must NOT list, then the Rulings Index and Decision Register (page 09), then this FAQ. Still open? Add a row to the question file with your proposed answer and keep working at the lower rating. The research lead batches questions for Heather's next gate and sends them through her decisions channel (page 09). Vendors never message Heather directly about research.

### Can I use this image?

Only if you opened and read its licence yourself. Allowed: public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, US government works. Not allowed: non-commercial, no-derivatives, any licence you didn't open, or a blank source line. A photo credited to an agency but posted under CC by someone else needs a second check. A living person with no free photo gets a name card. Record every image in the credits file. Full rules are on page 05 (Never-Break Rules and Legal).

### The magazine and the video disagree. Which one is right?

Neither one wins by default. The Fact Ledger is the master. Find the fact's ledger row, settle it there (with a ruling if it is disputed), then run the propagation checklist so both products match. (Proposed default DEC-2026-016, not yet approved.)

The research librarian runs every release. Only Heather approves one.
