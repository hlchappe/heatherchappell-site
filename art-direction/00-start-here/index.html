Start Here: How to Use the SIDE EYE Art Direction Standards

Version 1.1 draft · November 25, 2026 · Owner: Wren Hale, Onboarding and Handoff

Status: DRAFT for Heather's review

Midjourney rules follow the Brand Standards hub, section 11 (Midjourney), as of November 25, 2026. Where this document and section 11 differ, section 11 wins.

Who this is for: Anyone who designs, illustrates, lays out or prints a SIDE EYE issue: staff designers, outside vendors, illustrators, print vendors and AI agents.

Use it when: You are new to SIDE EYE, you are starting a new issue, or you are not sure which document answers your question.

Related: 01 Issue Design Process: From Brief to Print-Ready Files; 09 File Naming, Folders and Handoff Specs; 10 Vendor and Agent Onboarding Starter; 12 Decision Log; 13 Open Questions for Heather; SIDE EYE Brand Standards (the Brand Standards hub); Design System v3: Neon Zine Print

What SIDE EYE is

From the Brand Standards hub, section Brand core:

  • SIDE EYE is a history magazine and a YouTube show made by two hosts, Heather and Edwin. Tagline: History, allegedly.
  • It takes the stories everybody knows and finds the parts that fell out. It tests the conspiracy. It doesn't assume it.
  • The magazine has one theme per issue, prints at 8.5 × 11 in, and sells through Amazon KDP. Every image looks printed, pasted or carved by hand: neon ink on cream paper, never glossy.

Read Brand core in the hub before anything else. It takes five minutes.

What this set covers

This set is the art direction and production standard for the print magazine. It tells you how to take one issue from a brief to two print-ready PDFs (interior and cover) without asking anyone how things are done here.

It covers:

  • The design process, step by step, and who approves each step.
  • Cover, page and spread standards.
  • The grid and type system for the 8.5 × 11 KDP book.
  • Art direction, illustration and Midjourney prompts.
  • Image sourcing, rights and credits.
  • Print production and prepress for Amazon KDP.
  • Checklists, file naming, folders and handoff.
  • Vendor and AI agent onboarding, templates, decisions and open questions.

The golden rules

If you follow only these, you avoid the worst mistakes.

  1. Heather has the final yes. Nothing prints, posts or uploads, and nothing is marked Approved, without her sign-off. AI agents never mark anything Approved.
  2. The Scorecard binds the art. No image may show or suggest anything on a feature's Must NOT claim list, or any crime, deal or motive the Scorecard can't prove.
  3. One print process per image: RELIEF, COLLAGE, WOOD TYPE, HYBRID or NEWSPRINT. Don't invent a new look.
  4. In print, use black plus two inks per image and one pink focus per page. Keep total ink under 240%. Covers and the tabloid may use more inks.
  5. QUIET pages get no neon, no collage and no jokes. Serious subjects (deaths, atrocities, real suffering) are always QUIET.
  6. No generated lettering, anywhere. Every word is set in type or hand-lettered. Never generate the logo. Place it from the logo pack.
  7. Never name an artist, studio, shop or magazine in a prompt. Describe materials and methods only.
  8. Never recolor period art pink or neon. An illustration never imitates a period document or sits inside one. Illustrations carry the argument. Period images carry the proof.
  9. Every image needs a cleared rights line and a credit line before layout locks. No paid licenses.
  10. Real people: generated likenesses of historical figures are allowed, including photo-real period stills credited as photo illustrations. Living people appear only as caricature or print, never as a photoreal picture that could pass for a real photo, and only with sourced public facts. Living people are never shown doing something we can't prove.
  11. Respect the print limits: 7 pt minimum type, 300 dpi at placed size, and nothing essential within 0.5 in of the gutter.
  12. When sources disagree, don't pick. Use the source-of-truth order below. If that doesn't settle it, follow what the set says to follow and log the question.

How to ask a question

  1. Look first. Check the doc map above, then 12 Decision Log, then 13 Open Questions for Heather.
  2. Ask the issue's art director. The issue brief names the art director. If no one is named, ask Heather. Put the question in writing: in your handoff note, or in the issue's open items list.
  3. Write it so it can be answered in one word. Give the question, the options, what each option costs, and what you did in the meantime.
  4. Keep working. Don't stop the job for a question. Follow the safer option, or the rule this set names, and mark the work "pending answer."
  5. Standards-level questions (a rule that affects every issue) go to 13 Open Questions for Heather. Issue-level questions stay in that issue's Issue Hub doc.

Never settle a conflict by picking the rule you like. Write "Following X until Heather decides" in your notes, and keep going.
