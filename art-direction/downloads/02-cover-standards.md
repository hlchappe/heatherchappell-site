## 1. The short version

A SIDE EYE cover is one surreal picture that argues the issue's thesis. It is built as a COLLAGE in six layers. A photographic cut-out hero with oversized eyes leans in from one side and breaks the frame. The logo sits in a flat color field at the top with room around it. Four cover lines at most, set in typewriter mono on torn paper strips. The issue title runs down one edge, and the thesis line sits with it.

If you remember five rules:

1. One surreal idea per cover, and it must say the issue's thesis.
2. Never put any lettering in generated art. Every word is set in layout, and the logo is a placed file.
3. Four cover lines, maximum. Every line is true on the issue's fact-check log.
4. The hero comes in from the right on odd volumes and from the left on even volumes.
5. Build the wrap on KDP's own template for the final page count. Never guess the spine.

Heather has the final yes on every cover. No agent or vendor marks a cover approved.

## 2. Where covers sit in the issue process

The cover is made late on purpose. The thesis and the cover lines must be locked first, because the picture has to argue them.

| Step | What must be true before you start | Who signs off |
| :- | :- | :- |
| Thesis locked | The issue thesis is final in the Issue Hub | Heather |
| Cover lines picked | The headline pass is done and Heather picked four lines or fewer | Heather |
| Concept | One-sentence idea, hero, hero side and joke written down | Heather picks A or B |

| Type and logo | Masthead file placed, type set, nothing generated | Art director |

| Preflight | KDP previewer clean, cover checklist passed | Production, then Heather |

## 3. The cover concept rule

**Rule 3.1. One surreal idea.** Write the idea in one sentence before you make anything. If you need "and" to describe it, it is two ideas. Cut one.

**Rule 3.2. The picture argues the thesis.** A reader who sees only the picture should get the issue's point. Write one line that ties the picture to the thesis. Example from Vol. 1 (The Losers Issue, thesis "History belongs to whoever keeps the files"): a second-place podium in an empty wheat field, an old army truck, and an emu peeking in from the right. The joke: the emu won, and nobody gave it the podium.

**Rule 3.3. The joke must be true.** The cover is held to the same proof standard as the articles. It must not imply a crime, deal or motive that the issue's Scorecard rates below Proven. The Scorecard's "Must NOT claim" list binds the cover art too.

**Rule 3.4. Everyday things out of place.** The surreal idea comes from ordinary objects in the wrong place (a podium in a wheat field, an envelope over a swimming pool). Avoid fantasy scenes, space, glowing magic or anything that looks rendered on a screen.

**Rule 3.5. Write two concepts.** Concept A is the safer read. Concept B is the bolder joke. Heather picks one, or asks for another round.

**Rule 3.6. Who the hero can be.** The hero is an animal or an object with oversized eyes (the emu, the peacock). A historical figure may be the hero as a clear caricature, because generated likenesses of historical figures are allowed. Do not make a living person the cover hero. Anywhere else, living people appear only as caricature or print, never as a photo-real picture that could pass for a real photo, and only with sourced public facts (Brand Standards hub, section 11). The house rule is never to joke about anyone's looks. Any real person on a cover: ask Heather. (See 13 Open Questions for Heather if a future issue needs a living person on the cover.)

## 4. The COLLAGE build: six layers

Every cover is a COLLAGE (Design System v3: Neon Zine Print). Build it in these six layers, bottom to top. Keep each layer on its own layer or group in the layout file, so any part can be changed without regenerating the rest.

| Layer | What goes in it | How it is made | Vol. 1 example |
| :- | :- | :- | :- |
| 1. Ground | Cream or kraft paper, scraps of old print at the edges | Midjourney background or scanned paper | Aged paper, newsprint scraps at the bottom edge |
| 2. Scene | One real-looking photographic scene printed in flat silkscreen color fields | Midjourney background | Outback wheat field, flat blue sky, vintage truck, second-place podium |
| 3. Color fields | Flat neon blocks with hard edges; the top block holds the masthead | Midjourney blocks out the shape; you lay a flat vector fill in the house CMYK build over it | Flat pink block across the top third |
| 4. Marks | A loose painted neon brush border, a thin wobbly black ink frame, scanned handwritten margin notes | Midjourney for the border and frame; real scanned handwriting for notes | Pink brush border and black ink frame round the cover; handwriting down the left edge |
| 5. Hero | A photographic cut-out subject with exaggerated, oversized eyes, breaking the frame from one side | Midjourney hero on a white ground, cut out in Photoshop | The emu, peeking in from the right, eyes swiveled at the reader |
| 6. Cover lines | Short lines set in type on torn paper strips | Set in layout, printed or photographed on torn paper, pasted on | "Australia vs. 20,000 Emus" on a torn strip in typewriter mono (approved emu draft) |

Rules for the layers:

- **Rule 4.1.** Never mix RELIEF and COLLAGE in the cover frame. The HYBRID recipe is for feature openers, not covers.
- **Rule 4.2.** Covers may use more than black plus two inks. Use the house inks only (Toner Black, Side Eye Pink, Pale Cyan, Neon Orange, Acid Yellow, Tab Red, Tab Blue, Deep Navy, Peach for period skin, Warm Cream, Kraft). Builds are in the Brand Standards hub, section Color.
- **Rule 4.3.** Set every flat color field as a vector fill in its CMYK build (for example Side Eye Pink C0 M72 Y10 K0). Never rely on the pink that Midjourney painted. On Vol. 1 the masthead field came from the generated art and printed salmon, not Side Eye Pink.
- **Rule 4.4.** Keep total ink under 240% everywhere, including under the hero and in dark fields. Rich black for big fields is C50 M40 Y40 K100 (230%).
- **Rule 4.5.** Paper and texture go in the art around the logo, never on the logo.
- **Rule 4.6.** Beyond the pink masthead field and pink brush border that the v3 template uses, give pink to one thing only: the thing to see first. See the open question on pink in section 14.

## 5. Hero rules

**Rule 5.1. Breaks the frame.** The hero crosses the black ink frame and the brush border and runs off the trim on its side. On Vol. 1 the emu crosses the frame, overlaps the masthead field, and runs off the right edge and the bottom.

**Rule 5.2. Oversized eyes, looking at the reader.** The eyes are exaggerated and round, and they swivel toward the reader in a side-eye. The eyes are the first thing a shopper sees after the logo.

**Rule 5.3. Side alternates by volume.** Odd volumes: the hero leans in from the right. Even volumes: from the left. The covers then face each other on a shelf.

| Volume | Issue (as planned November 24, 2026) | Hero enters from |
| :- | :- | :- |
| Vol. 1 | The Losers Issue | Right |
| Vol. 2 | The Money Is Fake Issue | Left |
| Vol. 3 | The Mating Issue | Right |
| Vol. 4 | The Poison Issue | Left |

The issues were renumbered on November 24, 2026 (Money Is Fake moved ahead of Mating). Always go by the volume number in the Issue Hub, never by an older plan. Design System v2 shows a peacock entering from the left as "Vol. 2"; that peacock now belongs to Vol. 3, so it enters from the right.

**Rule 5.4. Keep the face safe.** The hero's eyes and face stay at least 0.25 in inside the trim and 0.375 in from the spine fold. Only the body, feathers, fur or the edge of an object may run off the page.

**Rule 5.5. Stay out of the logo's clear space.** The hero may overlap the masthead's color field. It never enters the logo's clear space (section 6).

**Rule 5.6. Cover lines never cross the eyes or face.** A strip may overlap a neck, a wing or a background.

**Rule 5.7. One hero.** No second animal, no crowd. Generate props for the hero separately and composite them if Midjourney drifts.

**Rule 5.8. Resolution.** The hero cut-out must be 300 dpi at placed size. Nothing below 200 dpi gets placed. Upscale before cutting out (Vol. 1 art used a 4x upscale), then sharpen after scaling.

## 6. Masthead and logo

The logo rules live in the Brand Standards hub, section Logo. Read it before you place the masthead. What a cover designer needs at hand:

**Rule 6.1. Place the file. Never rebuild it.** The masthead is the logo pack's horizontal lockup with tagline (file name pattern SIDE-EYE_horizontal-tagline_<color>), print CMYK PDF. Never retype SIDE EYE or HISTORY, ALLEGEDLY. in Anton or any font, and never ask Midjourney for the logo or the words. Vol. 1 was set before the logo pack existed and used typed Anton letters; do not copy that.

**Rule 6.2. Check the version.** Use the newest logo pack on the Brand Standards hub (version 1.1 as of November 24, 2026). Work already started may finish on the old version within 30 days.

**Rule 6.3. Position.** Put the masthead in the top color field, aligned to the outside and top live-area edges, with air around it. It is the highest element on the cover except an optional banner line (see the open question on banners in section 14).

**Rule 6.4. Clear space.** On a cover, leave the height of the eye (about 1.75X) on every side, where X is the width of the pink iris in the file you placed. The bare minimum is 1X. Measure from the eye's points, the letter edges and the tagline's comma. Nothing enters the clear space: no hero, no strip, no brush stroke, no busy picture detail.

**Rule 6.5. Size.** Make the masthead as wide as the top field allows after clear space. Never place the tagline lockup smaller than 60 mm wide (the print minimum). If you use the positive version (black and pink) on cream, it must be at least 132 mm wide, because the pink tagline is hard to read on light grounds.

**Rule 6.6. Which version on which field.** Pick the file by the field color. Never edit the file's colors.

| Top field color | Logo version | Never |
| :- | :- | :- |
| Side Eye Pink | Mono-black | A pink iris on pink |
| Tab Red | Mono-black | Pink |
| Tab Blue | Mono-white | Pink, black |
| Neon Orange, Acid Yellow, Pale Cyan, Kraft | Mono-black | Pink |
| Toner Black or Deep Navy | Reverse (cream and pink) | Positive |
| Warm Cream | Positive (black and pink), 132 mm wide or more | Reverse |
| Busy photo or art | The plate file (reverse on a black box) | Any version straight on the image |

**Rule 6.7.** The iris always glances right. Never flip, rotate, stretch, outline, shadow, glow or texture the logo.

## 7. Tagline, issue title and thesis line

**Rule 7.1. Tagline.** The tagline is "HISTORY, ALLEGEDLY." It is part of the masthead lockup file. Do not set it again anywhere on the front. The old tagline "History, with receipts." is retired; never use it.

**Rule 7.2. Issue title.** Copy the issue title exactly as written in the Issue Hub (for example THE MONEY IS FAKE ISSUE). Check that it matches the KDP listing title letter for letter, apostrophes included. Vol. 1 prints "THE LOSERS ISSUE" on the cover and is listed on Amazon as "The Loser's Issue"; do not repeat that mismatch.

**Rule 7.3. Issue title down one edge.** Set the issue title vertically down the edge opposite the hero, in Druk Condensed (Anton fallback), all caps. Read it top to bottom. (Design System v3 and v2; Vol. 1 printed it as a small horizontal label instead. See the open question in section 14.)

**Rule 7.4. Thesis line on every cover.** Every cover carries the issue thesis in one line, set in the body serif italic (Tiempos Text Italic, Source Serif Italic fallback), directly with the issue title. Copy it word for word from the Issue Hub. Vol. 1 printed without its thesis line; that was a miss.

| Vol. | Issue title | Thesis line (from the Issue Hub or plan) |
| :- | :- | :- |
| 1 | The Losers Issue | History belongs to whoever keeps the files. |
| 2 | The Money Is Fake Issue | Money is a promise. Someone always enforces it. (default in the Issue Hub until Heather says otherwise) |
| 3 | The Mating Issue | Who gets to choose? |

**Rule 7.5.** Type on the cover is 7 pt or larger, and every word is set in type or real scanned hand lettering. No "handwriting" fonts.

## 8. Cover lines

**Rule 8.1. Four at most.** The front carries four cover lines or fewer. A "Plus:" kicker or a banner line counts as one of the four. Past plans broke this every time: Addendum A planned six blurbs plus a kicker for Vol. 1, the printed Vol. 1 front has five lines, and the Mating Issue headline pass lists five lines plus a "Plus" line. Heather picks which four.

**Rule 8.2. Lines come after the thesis.** Write cover lines only after the thesis is locked. They come from the issue's headline pass.

**Rule 8.3. Short.** Keep each line to about 8 words and 40 characters. (The longest Vol. 1 line, RUPERT MURDOCH'S DAD AND THE EMU WAR, is 35 characters.) A line that needs two sentences is a deck, not a cover line.

**Rule 8.4. Typewriter mono on torn strips.** Set each line in typewriter mono (Courier Prime is the free stand-in) on a torn paper strip. Print or photograph the strip so the torn edge, paper grain and shadow are real, then paste it on in layout. Never generate the strip's words.

**Rule 8.5. Placement.** Put strips on the side opposite the hero, at angles of a few degrees, never across the hero's eyes or face, never in the logo's clear space, and at least 0.25 in inside the trim (0.375 in from the spine fold).

**Rule 8.6. True and in voice.** Every line is a bold, arguable claim backed by the piece's own text and logged in the fact-check log. No overstatement (the first Mating draft said Nettie Stevens "found the sex chromosomes," which overstates her work). No lines that announce the research ("We Read the Footnotes"). No "receipts" as a metaphor, no "It's not X, it's Y" lines, and no promises of things KDP can't print (perforations, flaps, inside covers, fold-outs, scratch-and-sniff).

**Rule 8.7. Case.** Use the wording and capitalization Heather approved in the headline pass. Do not restyle them on the cover.

## 9. Volume number, date, price

**Rule 9.1. Volume number.** Volumes are numbered in release order. Take the number from the Issue Hub on the day you build the cover. Write it as VOL. N (Vol. 1 spine: "VOL. 1").

**Rule 9.2. Where it goes.** Put the volume number on the spine when the spine carries text (section 10) and in the back-cover identity line (section 11). The front has no agreed place for it yet; see Gaps in the notes and 13 Open Questions for Heather.

**Rule 9.3. Cover date.** If a cover date is used, copy it from the Issue Hub (for example Feb/Mar 2027 for Vol. 3). Never guess one from the content calendar, which was written before the renumbering.

**Rule 9.4. No price on the cover.** Amazon sets and shows the price. Vol. 1 printed no price. Do not add one unless Heather asks.

## 10. Spine

The spine width comes from the final interior page count. Premium color on white paper: page count × 0.002347 in.

**Rule 10.1. KDP's text limit.** KDP allows spine text only on books with more than 79 pages. Under that, the spine is a solid color with no text at all, not even a number.

**Rule 10.2. Spine margins.** Keep at least 0.0625 in between spine text and each fold. Nothing on the spine may need to line up exactly with a fold, because the fold can drift 0.0625 in either way.

**Rule 10.3. Text size.** Spine text is 7 pt or larger. Take the room for text as the spine width minus 0.125 in. If that room is under about 0.1 in, 7 pt caps won't fit cleanly; leave the spine without text.

| Interior pages | Spine width (in) | Room for text (in) | Spine text? |
| :- | :- | :- | :- |
| Under 80 | under 0.188 | none | No (KDP) |
| 80 | 0.188 | 0.063 | Allowed by KDP, too narrow for 7 pt; no text |
| 96 | 0.225 | 0.100 | Just fits 7 pt caps |
| 112 | 0.263 | 0.138 | Yes |
| 120 | 0.282 | 0.157 | Yes |
| 132 | 0.310 | 0.185 | Yes |

The planned range for an issue is 116 to 132 pages.

**Rule 10.4. What the spine says.** Until Heather decides (open question, section 14), follow the printed Vol. 1 spine: SIDE EYE · VOL. N · THE <TITLE> ISSUE in mono caps, cream on a Toner Black field, reading top to bottom, centered on the spine. Never place the logo lockup on a spine this narrow; it falls under its minimum size.

**Rule 10.5. Color across the fold.** Run the front and back backgrounds at least 0.0625 in past each fold into the spine, or pick a spine color that matches the neighboring cover edge, so fold drift never shows a sliver of the wrong color.

## 11. Back cover

The back cover is one of the "money pages": everyone reads it, including people who never open the issue. It must read in under 15 seconds, make one point, and have no text block over 120 words.

**Rule 11.1. Concept.** Until Heather decides (open question, section 14), the back cover is the full-page parody ad planned for that volume in Front and Back Matter, Vols 1-3 (The Money Pages): Vol. 2, The First Bank of Yap; Vol. 3, The Train. Vol. 1 printed a toy-ad parody ("TIRED OF WINNING? CALL THE LOSERS SQUAD!") instead of the planned catalog ad. Design System v3's recipe for the back cover is a RELIEF colorway sheet (one motif repeated in every ink combination); use a colorway sheet only if Heather picks it.

**Rule 11.2. Art on the back.** Build the ad's art in one of the five processes (most often RELIEF or WOOD TYPE), with no generated lettering. Historical figures may appear as generated caricature or toy-style likenesses (Vol. 1 did this with Napoleon, Hatshepsut and Hannibal). Living people may not appear in a way that suggests they did something the issue can't prove. A living person who appears at all is only a caricature or print, never a photo-real picture, and needs Heather's OK first (06, section 9).

**Rule 11.3. Identity line.** Set one small mono line near the bottom: SIDE EYE Vol. N · The <Title> Issue · History, allegedly. (Vol. 1 did this.) Copy the title exactly as on the front.

**Rule 11.4. Elevator pitch.** Brand Identity: Name, Tagline and Pitch says the pitch belongs on the back cover: "You were right to suspect the story you were told was bullshit. The good news is the truth is more interesting." Vol. 1 left it off. Include it unless Heather says otherwise (open question, section 14).

**Rule 11.5. Barcode zone.** KDP prints the barcode on the back cover in a white box 2 in wide by 1.2 in tall, in the lower right corner, beside the spine. Keep that zone empty: no text, no faces, no key art.

| Item | Value |
| :- | :- |
| Barcode box | 2.0 in wide × 1.2 in tall |
| Position | Lower right corner of the back cover, next to the spine |
| From the spine fold | 0.25 in (Vol. 1 measured 0.26 in) |
| From the bottom trim | 0.25 in (Vol. 1 measured 0.27 in) |
| What wins | The barcode box on KDP's downloaded template for your page count |

Vol. 1 drew its own white box in this zone. If you draw one, match the template box exactly and put nothing under its edges.

**Rule 11.6. Bleed on the back.** Run the back cover's background 0.125 in past the top, bottom and outside trim. On Vol. 1 the black field stopped at the trim and the bleed was cream, so any cutting drift shows a cream sliver.

**Rule 11.7. Text safety.** All back-cover text sits at least 0.25 in inside the trim and 0.375 in from the spine fold. Minimum 7 pt.

**Rule 11.8. No inside covers.** KDP paperbacks don't print the inside of the cover. Never plan art, answers, collectibles or a star chart there. There are no flaps either.

## 12. Cover file spec (KDP full wrap)

One PDF holds the back cover, spine and front cover as one spread. Full rules for export and upload are in 07 Print Production and Prepress (Amazon KDP).

**The size formula.** Trim is 8.5 × 11 in. Bleed is 0.125 in.

- Width = 0.125 + 8.5 (back) + spine + 8.5 (front) + 0.125 = **17.25 in + spine**
- Height = 0.125 + 11 + 0.125 = **11.25 in**
- Spine = **interior page count × 0.002347 in** (premium color on white)

| Interior pages | Spine (in) | Full wrap (in) | Pixels at 300 dpi |
| :- | :- | :- | :- |
| 96 | 0.2253 | 17.4753 × 11.25 | 5,243 × 3,375 |
| 112 | 0.2629 | 17.5129 × 11.25 | 5,254 × 3,375 |
| 116 | 0.2723 | 17.5223 × 11.25 | 5,257 × 3,375 |
| 120 | 0.2816 | 17.5316 × 11.25 | 5,259 × 3,375 |
| 124 | 0.2910 | 17.5410 × 11.25 | 5,262 × 3,375 |
| 128 | 0.3004 | 17.5504 × 11.25 | 5,265 × 3,375 |
| 132 | 0.3098 | 17.5598 × 11.25 | 5,268 × 3,375 |

**Rule 12.1. Template first.** Download KDP's cover template for the exact final page count, trim and paper, and build on it. If the template and this table disagree, the template wins. If the page count changes by even two pages, download a new template and rebuild the wrap.

**Rule 12.2. Front panel art.** The front panel with bleed is 8.625 × 11.25 in, so front art needs at least 2,588 × 3,375 px. Use 300 dpi at placed size.

**Rule 12.3. Zones on each panel.**

| Zone | Front | Back |
| :- | :- | :- |
| Bleed | 0.125 in past top, bottom and outside trim | Same |
| Text and faces | 0.25 in inside top, bottom and outside trim | Same |
| Spine side | 0.375 in from the spine fold (0.25 in plus 0.125 in for spine shift) | Same |
| Barcode | none | 2 × 1.2 in box, lower right by the spine |

**Rule 12.4. Export.**

- CMYK only. Convert every RGB image before export. No spot colors.
- 300 dpi images, flattened transparency, all fonts embedded.
- No crop marks, registration marks, color bars or template guides.
- Matte finish.
- One PDF. KDP suggests 40 MB or less.
- Open the font list in the PDF before upload. Only house fonts or their listed stand-ins may appear. Vol. 1's cover PDF came out of a browser in RGB and carried a fallback font (DejaVu Sans Mono) for a missing glyph.

**Rule 12.5. Keep a clean art file.** Save the cover art with no type in it. Vol. 1's cover art image has an old "SIDE EYE" masthead and an old cover-line strip baked into the picture, with new type pasted over them. That locks old words into the art. Keep type on its own live layers.

**Rule 12.6. File names and handoff.** Name and file the cover as 09 File Naming, Folders and Handoff Specs says. The precedent is SIDE-EYE-Vol1-Cover-KDP-final.pdf in the issue's "to print" folder. The final cover PDF in that folder is the only cover anyone may use downstream (YouTube end screens, listings, ads). Never use a draft cover file.

## 13. Midjourney prompts for covers

The house prompt templates, settings and fixes live in 05 Art and Illustration Direction (with Midjourney Prompt Standards). This section gives the cover pattern only. The Brand Standards hub, section 11 (Midjourney), sets the Midjourney rules, and it wins wherever 05 or this section differs from it.

**The cover is two generations, then a composite.** Generate the background (no hero) and the hero separately. Cut the hero out in Photoshop and composite it in layout.

**Standing rules for every cover prompt:**

- Use `--raw` on every prompt. It replaces `--style raw`, the old spelling, which no cover prompt uses any more.
- Every prompt carries `--v 8.2`. Put the settings in this order: `--ar W:H --raw --s #`, then `--hd`, then `--sref` and `--sw` (backgrounds only, once the address exists), then `--v 8.2`, then the `--no` list last.
- Add `--hd` to every cover prompt, background and hero alike, because covers are full-page art.
- Stylize: photographs take `--s 50` to `--s 100`. Prints and illustration take `--s 100` to `--s 250`. Never go above `--s 300`. The collage background is print-style, so it takes `--s 250`. The hero cut-out is a photograph, so it takes `--s 75`.
- Background: `--ar 17:22` (the trim's shape), with the approved emu cover as `--sref` at `--sw 30`. No other cover prompt takes a style reference.
- The approved emu cover doesn't have a Midjourney image address yet (Midjourney can't open a Google Drive file). Until the Midjourney desk has one, background prompts run complete without `--sref` and `--sw`. Once the address exists, add `--sref` with that address and `--sw 30` right before `--v 8.2`. Never put a placeholder in a prompt.
- Hero: `--ar 1:2`, no style reference, against a plain white backdrop.
- Never write `--sw` without `--sref`. Never use a moodboard.
- Switched off for cover art: Personalization (`--p`, and turn it off in your Midjourney settings too), `--weird`, `--exp`, `--q`, `--sv`, `--oref`, `--ow`, `--cref` and `::` weights. Chaos stays at 0 for finals, so leave `--c` out of a final prompt.
- Every prompt carries a `--no` list that includes text, letters, words, numbers, logo, watermark. The background is print-style, so its list also adds glow, gradient, chrome, 3d render, glossy, plus animals and people (the hero is generated separately). The hero is a photograph, so its list adds full body, HDR, airbrushed skin, bokeh.
- No gloss words in any prompt: photorealistic, cinematic, 8k, masterpiece, ultra realistic, glowing, epic. Name the real capture method and the real light instead, and add two or three real imperfections. The collage is a hand-made paper collage photographed on a copy stand under daylight lamps, with its scene printed from a real photograph in flat silkscreen color fields. The hero is a real photograph with oversized eyes, made with a camera, film and light true to the story's era, so describe how it was photographed, not how real it looks.
- Source or inspiration image URLs go in the prompt: image prompts go first, before the words, and a style reference goes after `--sref`. Google Drive links don't work in Midjourney. Use the address Midjourney gives the image (copy it from the Midjourney archive, or upload the file in the prompt bar and copy the address it gives). Which images may be used this way is set in 06 Imagery Sourcing, Rights and Credits (section 7).
- Never name an artist, studio, shop or magazine. Describe materials and methods. Film stocks and camera models may be named as materials.
- Never ask for text. Every paper scrap in the collage is "completely blank." No prompt may ask for the SIDE EYE logo, the eye mark or the words SIDE EYE. If a result shows lettering or an eye logo, reject it.
- People: the hero is an animal, an object or a historical caricature (Rule 3.6). No living person is ever the hero. Historical figures may also appear as generated likenesses. Any real person on a cover: ask Heather before you generate anything.
- Give three prompts per image: A is the safe read, B is the bold joke, C is a different take. Each one is complete and ready to copy and paste, one per code block, with no placeholders. These three versions are not the same as concepts A and B in Rule 3.5: once Heather picks a concept, every image in it gets an A, a B and a C prompt.

## 15. Cover approval steps

1. **Brief.** Pull the issue title, volume number, thesis line, cover lines (four or fewer) and cover date from the Issue Hub. Confirm Heather has picked the lines.
2. **Concept.** Write concept A and concept B: the one-sentence idea, the tie to the thesis, the hero, the hero side (by volume), the top field color and the joke. Check each against the Scorecard's "Must NOT claim" list.
3. **Heather picks a concept.** No generating at full scale before this.
4. **Generate.** Run the A, B and C prompts for the background and the hero (section 13). Log them.
5. **Comp.** Build the front at full size: art layers, flat vector fields, placed logo file, set type, torn-strip cover lines.
6. **Self-check.** Run the cover checklist (section 17).
7. **Independent review.** A second reviewer (person or agent) checks the comp against this document and the Scorecard. Covers are high-stakes pieces.
8. **Heather reviews the front.** Show it at full size and at thumbnail size, the way a shopper sees it on Amazon. Heather approves or sends changes.
9. **Build the wrap.** After the interior page count is final, download the KDP template, build spine and back cover, and export the PDF.
10. **Preflight.** Run KDP's online previewer and fix every flag. Order a proof copy when Heather has approved one for the issue (neon builds can't be judged on screen).
11. **Final yes.** Heather approves the final cover PDF before upload. File it in the issue's "to print" folder.

## 17. Cover checklist

Run it on the front alone, then on the full wrap. Every box must be ticked before Heather sees the final.

**Concept**
- [ ] One surreal idea, written in one sentence
- [ ] The picture argues the issue's thesis
- [ ] Nothing in the art implies a claim the Scorecard rates below Proven
- [ ] Hero is an animal, an object or a historical caricature; no living person as hero
- [ ] Any real person on the cover approved by Heather

**Art**
- [ ] Six COLLAGE layers, each on its own layer or group
- [ ] Hero has oversized eyes looking at the reader
- [ ] Hero breaks the frame and enters from the correct side for this volume (odd right, even left)
- [ ] Hero's eyes and face 0.25 in inside the trim and 0.375 in from the spine fold
- [ ] Flat color fields are vector fills in house CMYK builds
- [ ] No generated lettering or logo anywhere in the art
- [ ] Every prompt follows section 13: A, B and C versions, `--raw`, the right `--s` band, `--v 8.2`, a `--no` list and no gloss words
- [ ] Art is 300 dpi at placed size; front art at least 2,588 × 3,375 px
- [ ] Total ink under 240% everywhere

**Logo and type**
- [ ] Masthead is the placed horizontal-tagline file from the current logo pack
- [ ] Logo version matches the field color (section 6 table)
- [ ] Clear space of one eye height on every side; nothing inside it
- [ ] Issue title matches the Issue Hub and the KDP listing exactly
- [ ] Issue title runs down the edge opposite the hero
- [ ] Thesis line present, word for word, in serif italic
- [ ] Four cover lines or fewer, counting any banner or Plus line
- [ ] Each line about 8 words or fewer, in typewriter mono on a torn strip
- [ ] Every line is on the fact-check log and passes the voice rules
- [ ] No strip crosses the hero's eyes or face or the logo's clear space
- [ ] All type 7 pt or larger, 0.25 in inside the trim, 0.375 in from the spine fold
- [ ] No price on the cover

**Spine**
- [ ] Spine width = final page count × 0.002347 in, matching the KDP template
- [ ] No spine text under 80 pages, or when the room for text is under about 0.1 in
- [ ] Spine text 0.0625 in clear of both folds, reading top to bottom
- [ ] Volume number on the spine matches the Issue Hub

**Back cover**
- [ ] Concept matches the approved plan for this volume
- [ ] Reads in under 15 seconds; no block over 120 words
- [ ] Identity line present; elevator pitch present unless Heather said no
- [ ] Barcode zone (2 × 1.2 in, lower right by the spine) empty
- [ ] Background bleeds 0.125 in past top, bottom and outside trim
- [ ] Nothing planned for inside covers or flaps

**File**
- [ ] Built on KDP's template for the final page count
- [ ] Width = 17.25 in + spine; height 11.25 in
- [ ] CMYK, no spot colors, transparency flattened, fonts embedded, house fonts only
- [ ] No crop marks, color bars or template guides
- [ ] KDP previewer shows no flags
- [ ] Every image on the cover has a credit line in the colophon
- [ ] Heather's written yes recorded before upload
