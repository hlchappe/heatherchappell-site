## 1. The process on one page

Every issue moves through nine stages, in order. Each stage ends at a gate. A gate passes only with Heather's recorded yes, or, where this document says so, the art director's.

| # | Stage | What it produces | Who passes the gate | Checklist in 08 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Issue Brief | The approved Issue Brief | Heather | Stage 0 Issue Brief |
| 1 | Flatplan | The approved page-by-page flatplan | Heather | Stage 1 Flatplan |
| 2 | Art Direction | One art-direction doc per feature, plus cover and departments | Heather (cover and concepts); art director (revisions inside an approved concept) | Stage 2 Art Direction |
| 3 | Art Production | Final image files, a complete art log and a rights log with every placed image Cleared | Art director (picks each image); Heather (cover, openers, real people) | Stage 3 Art Production |
| 4 | Layout | A full review PDF of interior and cover | Art director (ready for review) | Stage 4 Layout |
| 5 | Review and Legal Read | A signed-off layout, the legal read checklist and a fixed review PDF | Heather | Stage 5 Review and Legal Read |
| 6 | Prepress | Print-ready interior and cover PDFs that pass KDP's previewer | Production (preflight); Heather (upload) | Stage 6 Prepress |
| 7 | Proof Copy | A proof report and a yes to publish | Heather | Stage 7 Proof Copy |
| 8 | Release and Archive | The live book, the release note and the archived issue package | Heather (publish) | Stage 8 Release and Archive |

Three rules hold across every stage:

1. **Heather has the final yes.** Nothing ships, prints, posts or costs money without her sign-off (Brand Standards hub, "The golden rules").
2. **Agents never mark work Approved or Published.** An agent moves work up to a gate and says it is ready. Only Heather marks Approved (Brand Standards hub, "What always needs Heather's yes").
3. **Nothing counts as delivered until it is in Drive,** in the issue folder that doc 09 names, under the name doc 09 sets.

The status of each issue (which stage, who is on it, dates, who approved each gate and when) lives in **the production tracker**. This set of standards says how the work is done. The tracker says where the work stands. Never track status inside a standards doc.

## 2. Roles

Use these role names in every brief, flatplan, log and handoff note.

| Role | Does | Can an AI agent fill it? | Can a human vendor fill it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Publisher / Editor in Chief (Heather) | Sets the issue, approves every gate, spends money, uploads to KDP, publishes | No | No |
| Art Director (AD) | Owns the look of the issue; writes the brief draft, flatplan and art-direction docs; approves individual images; runs internal checks | Yes, with the side-eye-art-direction skill | Yes |
| Designer / Layout | Builds pages and the cover to docs 03, 04 and 07; places art, captions and credits; makes review PDFs | Yes | Yes |
| Illustrator / Image Generator | Runs Midjourney prompts, picks and upscales results, edits and composites images | Writes prompts, reviews results and edits files. A person runs Midjourney itself (see note) | Yes |
| Rights Checker | Opens every source page, reads the license line, fills and signs the rights log | Yes, if it can open the source page itself | Yes |
| Fact-check / Research | Hands over the research package; checks every new fact, number, caption and display line added in design | Yes, as an independent checker in a fresh session | Yes |
| Production | Prepress, KDP preflight, proof checks, final files, archive package | Yes, except anything inside the KDP account | Yes, except anything inside the KDP account |

**Note on Midjourney.** In Vol. 1 and Vol. 2 the prompts were written by agents and run by hand in Midjourney by a person on SIDE EYE's account. Keep it that way: Midjourney is run by hand, never by automation. Who runs it for a given issue is named in the Issue Brief.

**Note on co-editing.** Heather may bring Edwin into any review. His notes go to the AD through Heather's review notes, and Heather's yes is still the one that counts.

**Conflicts of interest.** Nobody checks their own work. The person who sourced an image does not sign its rights row when someone else is available. The fact-checker on design text did not write that text. The legal read is run by someone who did not write the piece (Research Guidelines Portal plan, DEC-2026-005).

## 4. How editorial hands off to design

Design does not invent or check facts. It works from what editorial hands over. The handoff happens per feature (so art direction can start early) and then for the whole issue (so layout can start).

### Per feature, before Stage 2 starts on it

- **Text locked.** The feature's text is approved by Heather. Status LOCKED in the file name or header.
- **Scorecard with the Must NOT claim list.** Every rated claim, plus the list of things the piece must never say or imply. The Must NOT list binds the art as much as the text: art never implies a crime, deal or motive that the Scorecard rates below Proven (side-eye-art-direction skill).
- **Research package accepted.** The package from the research process (Fact Ledger, sources doc, rulings, Living Person Register where living people appear), accepted by Heather at the research hand-off gate (R8 in the Research Guidelines Portal plan).
- **Register and tab.** LOUD or QUIET, and which of the issue's three tabs the piece files under.
- **Word count** for the text and each sidebar.

### For the whole issue, before Stage 4 starts

- The full manuscript is LOCKED, including the opening letter, departments, captions written by editorial, cover lines and the issue thesis line.
- The fact-check log is closed: zero open items, or each one signed by Heather.
- The back-of-issue sources section text exists and matches the fact-check log one to one (Brand Standards hub, "What's in every issue").
- The headline pass is done.

### What design does when a handoff item is missing

Do not start the stage for that piece. Log it in the production tracker as blocked, name the missing item, and work on another piece. Never fill a gap with your own facts, quotes, numbers or dates.

### New facts found during design

Any fact, number, date or quote that design adds (an infographic figure, a caption, a pull quote, a map pin, a cover line) goes back to Fact-check / Research as a change request. It gets a ledger row, a rating and a check before it prints (Research Guidelines Portal plan, section 7.1). Packaging is rated too: cover lines and headlines follow the same scale as the body.

## 5. The stages in detail

### Stage 0: Issue Brief

**Purpose.** Fix what the issue is, what it costs and when it ships, before anyone designs a page.

**Inputs (must exist before you start).**
- The issue's plan in the content calendar (theme, thesis, three tabs, lineup).
- A lineup with a status and rough word count for every piece.
- A proposed launch (on-sale) date from Heather.
- The issue's research tier list (which features run the full research process).

**Steps.**
1. Copy the Issue Brief template (doc 11) into the issue's plan folder.
2. Fill theme, thesis line, audience, volume number and launch date from the calendar and Heather's notes. Use the newest Heather decision where the calendar and the manuscript disagree, and say which you used.
3. Set the page count target. It must be even. Plan 116 to 132 pages (Design System v2 Addendum A). Over 150 pages changes the gutter and the grid, so flag it.
4. List the features with tab, register and word count. Confirm the issue rules: at least one QUIET feature; at least two stories centered on women and two from outside Europe (content calendar, "Rules every volume follows").
5. Copy in the issue's Must NOT claim list: the combined Must NOT lines from every feature's Scorecard, plus issue-level flags from the calendar.
6. Write the hero idea: one surreal cover idea, and the cover side. The hero alternates sides by volume (Vol. 1 right, Vol. 2 left; Brand Standards hub, "What's in every issue"). Doc 02 says which side each volume takes.
7. List every item KDP cannot print and how it will be redrawn: no gatefolds, inserts, die cuts, perforations or spot inks (Addendum A, sections 1 and 5).
8. Fill the budget lines: proof copies (number and who orders them), and confirm zero paid image licenses.
9. Name who fills each role, and who runs Midjourney.
10. Send the brief to Heather with a Handoff Note.

**Outputs.** The approved Issue Brief, filed in the issue's 01 Plan folder (doc 09 sets the name).

**Who.** AD drafts. Fact-check / Research supplies the Must NOT list. Heather approves.

**Gate.** Heather approves the thesis, page count target, launch date, cover side, proof budget and role list. The AD cannot pass this gate.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 0 Issue Brief.

### Stage 1: Flatplan

**Purpose.** Decide what goes on every page and spread before any art is made.

**Inputs.**
- The approved Issue Brief.
- The lineup with word counts.
- The standard running order (Up Front, The Files, Out Back) from the content calendar's standard issue template.
- The page recipes in Design System v3 and doc 03.

**Steps.**
1. Lay out the running order spread by spread. Keep the standard order so readers know where things live.
2. For every page, set: section, recipe, register (LOUD or QUIET), tab, print process (RELIEF, COLLAGE, WOOD TYPE, HYBRID or NEWSPRINT), owner and status. Use the Flatplan template (doc 11).
3. Copyfit. LOUD body runs 3 columns, QUIET body 2 columns (Addendum A, section 2). As a starting estimate, a LOUD feature with art carries about 450 words a page (the Napoleon feature needed 20 pages for about 9,050 words). Check the estimate against a test page before you lock.
4. Start features on a left-hand page when the opener is a spread, so each pair is a true spread.
5. Apply the binding rules: the first and last spreads do not open flat, so no art there depends on it. Any cut-out page goes on a right-hand page with a sacrificial back (Addendum A, sections 5 and 6).
6. Mark first cuts: the pieces you drop if the count runs over.
7. Check the total is even and matches the brief. If it doesn't, file a Change Request (section 7).

**Outputs.** The approved flatplan (a Google Sheet), filed in 01 Plan (doc 09 sets the name).

**Who.** AD with Designer / Layout. Heather approves.

**Gate.** Heather approves the page count and the running order. After that, the AD may move pieces between spreads, swap recipes or rebalance pages as long as the page count, the running order and the cover stay the same. Log each move in the flatplan's change notes.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 1 Flatplan.

### Stage 2: Art Direction

**Purpose.** Decide the visual idea for every page and write the instructions that make it, before any image is generated or bought.

**Inputs.**
- The approved flatplan.
- For each feature: its LOCKED text and its Scorecard with the Must NOT list (section 4).
- Design System v3: Neon Zine Print, the v2 grid rules, and the model deliverable: the Napoleon Art Direction doc.
- Docs 02, 03, 05 and 06 of this set.

**Steps.**
1. Write one art-direction doc per feature, and one each for the cover, Up Front and Out Back. Use the Art-Direction Doc template (doc 11), which follows the Napoleon model.
2. State the concept in a sentence tied to the piece's thesis. Name the joke and the one thing pink marks (one pink focus per page in print).
3. Pick one process per image. HYBRID only for real historical people. QUIET pages get no neon, no collage and no jokes.
4. Write two prompts per generated image: A the safer read, B the bolder joke (doc 05). No artist, studio, shop or magazine names. No generated lettering: describe paper and signs as "completely blank".
5. List each historical image the piece needs with its proposed source and rights basis. Paid licenses are not allowed (Brand Standards hub, "Image rights"). Mark each as a candidate for the Rights Checker.
6. List everything built by hand in layout: wood-type plates, cover lines, charts, maps, timelines, ornaments.
7. Check every image idea against the Must NOT list. Art never implies a crime, deal or motive the Scorecard can't prove. Living people follow the sourced-public-facts-only rule. Generated likenesses of historical figures are allowed.
8. For the cover and for any opener about a living person, have an independent reviewer (someone who did not write the doc) check the plan against doc 05's checklist and the Scorecard.
9. Send the set to Heather with a Handoff Note.

**Outputs.** One art-direction doc per feature, plus cover, Up Front and Out Back docs, filed in 04 Art > 01 Briefs. Each starts its rows in the issue's art log and rights log (doc 11; doc 05 section 12 and doc 06 section 5 set the columns).

**Who.** AD (agent with the side-eye-art-direction skill, or a human AD). Illustrator and Rights Checker consulted on what is feasible. Heather approves.

**Gate.** Heather approves the cover concept and each feature's concept. After that, the AD may revise prompts, swap an image for another with the same process and rights status, or change a crop without asking, as long as the concept, the joke and what pink marks stay the same.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 2 Art Direction.

### Stage 3: Art Production

**Purpose.** Make, source, clear and finish every image the flatplan needs.

**Inputs.**
- Approved art-direction docs.
- The art log and rights log started in Stage 2.
- The two style references named in Design System v3 (the powdered-wig skull print and the approved emu cover).

**Steps.**
1. Run the prompts in Midjourney by hand. Record each run in the art log: prompt as run, A or B, generator and version, job ID and grid position, date and file name (doc 05, section 12).
2. Generate the hardest or most important image first to lock the look (the Napoleon doc locked the opener first, then ran the cast set in one sitting).
3. Pick variants against the art-direction doc. Reject anything with stray lettering, stray faces on hands-only scenes, glow, chrome or gradients (doc 05 has the fixes).
4. Upscale and edit to final size: 300 dpi at placed size; a full-bleed page needs 2,588 × 3,375 px; nothing under 200 dpi is placed at any size (Addendum A, section 8).
5. Source historical images. The Rights Checker opens every source page, reads the license line, saves the rights statement as evidence, and fills the rights log row. Only a Cleared row can go to print; Pending can sit in a layout comp; Blocked is never placed (doc 06, section 5).
6. Composite HYBRID figures and cut-outs. Never recolor period art pink or neon. Never put an illustration inside a period document, and never make generated art look like a period print or record.
7. Convert finished images to CMYK for print. Keep total ink under 240% (Brand Standards hub, "Print spec").
8. Name and file each image under its image ID (doc 09).

**Outputs.** Final image files in 04 Art > 09 Finals, named per doc 09; the art log complete; the rights log with every placed image Cleared and every credit line written.

**Who.** Illustrator / Image Generator and Rights Checker do the work. The AD picks. Heather approves the cover, every opener and every image of a real person.

**Gate.** The AD picks each image against its art-direction doc and the Stage 3 checklist (art log status Picked). Heather approves the cover, every opener, every image of a real person and anything flagged in an art-direction doc's open items; she may approve the rest in one pass on the art-direction doc (doc 05, section 12.4). Only Heather, or the person she names, fills the art log's "Approved by and date" column. An image without a Cleared rights row may sit in layout as a marked placeholder, but it cannot reach Stage 6.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 3 Art Production.

### Stage 4: Layout

**Purpose.** Build every page and the cover to spec, with locked text and finished art.

**Inputs.**
- The LOCKED manuscript and closed fact-check log (section 4).
- The approved flatplan.
- Finished art, or marked placeholders.
- Fonts: the free stand-ins named in doc 04 until the paid faces have a recorded license.

**Steps.**
1. Build on the 12-column grid and 13 pt baseline, at the type sizes in doc 04 and the page recipes in doc 03.
2. Pick one of the three layout routes in doc 09, section 4: a packaged layout file (InDesign or Affinity Publisher), an HTML build (the Vol. 1 route: HTML and CSS printed to PDF), or a page spec for agents who can't run layout software. Any route is fine if the output meets doc 07 and the job can be rebuilt from the package.
3. Place text exactly as locked. Never cut or rewrite text to make it fit. If it doesn't fit, file a Change Request to editorial with the overage in lines.
4. Set captions and credit lines from the art-direction docs and the rights log. Build the colophon credits (one Midjourney credit, image credits, influence credits).
5. Send every display line added in layout (headlines if changed, pull quotes, chart numbers, map pins, cover lines) to Fact-check / Research for a check.
6. Export a review PDF of the interior and one of the cover. Name them as builds (doc 09), so old builds never get mistaken for the latest.
7. Run the Stage 4 checklist and fix everything it finds before Heather sees the PDF.

**Outputs.** Interior and cover review PDFs in 05 Layout > Exports, build-numbered per doc 09, plus the layout source files.

**Who.** Designer / Layout builds. AD reviews. Fact-check / Research checks display text.

**Gate.** The AD marks the build ready for review. This is an internal gate; the next gate is Heather's.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 4 Layout.

### Stage 5: Review and Legal Read

**Purpose.** Get Heather's yes on the whole issue as it will print, with the legal check run last. Understanding comes first; the legal check comes last (Brand Standards hub, "How a feature gets made").

**Inputs.**
- The ready-for-review interior and cover PDFs.
- The issue's Must NOT list, Living Person Register and rights log.
- The fact-check results on display text.

**Steps.**
1. Heather reads the full layout and returns notes. Plan two working days for this read.
2. The AD sorts notes into fixes (no approval needed beyond Heather's note) and Change Requests (anything that changes page count, the cover, locked text or the running order).
3. Run any sensitivity or expert reads the brief calls for.
4. Run the issue-level rights check (doc 06, section 14): every placed image Cleared, every credit line in place, the credit list matching the rights log one to one, and no Pending or Blocked row placed. This is the "rights check before print" that confirms each Cleared row.
5. Apply fixes. Export the near-final build.
6. Run the legal read last, on the near-final build. The legal read is a checklist run by someone who did not write the piece. Any line or image that frames a living person as doing wrong is softened to court and filing language, or cut (Research Guidelines Portal plan, DEC-2026-005). Check the Must NOT list line by line against text and art.
7. Apply any legal fixes. Export the next build. Heather confirms the fixes.

**Outputs.** Heather's review notes; the signed legal read checklist; the signed rights check; the fixed review PDFs. File them in 06 Reviews and Legal.

**Who.** Heather reads. AD and Designer fix. Fact-check / Research runs the legal checklist. The Rights Checker runs the rights check.

**Gate.** Heather signs off text and layout for prepress. After this, only fixes found in Stage 6 or 7 are allowed, each by Change Request.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 5 Review and Legal Read.

### Stage 6: Prepress

**Purpose.** Turn the approved layout into files KDP will print without surprises.

**Inputs.**
- The Stage 5 approved build.
- The final page count, and KDP's cover template downloaded for that count.

**Steps.** (Doc 07 holds the full spec. The short version, from Addendum A and the hub:)
1. Export the interior as one PDF at 8.625 × 11.25 in (trim plus bleed), single pages in reading order, fonts embedded, no crop or registration marks, transparency flattened, all images CMYK.
2. Check bleed (0.125 in on outside edges, none on the gutter), interior text safety (0.375 in inside trim, KDP's minimum; doc 07) and the 7 pt type minimum. Cover safety is set in doc 02.
3. Check ink: total 240% or less (doc 07). Use the rich black the hub names (see doc 07).
4. Build the cover on KDP's template for the final page count. Spine width is page count × 0.002347 in.
5. Upload both files to the KDP draft. Only Heather, or someone she has given access, does this step.
6. Run KDP's Print Previewer and fix every flagged page.
7. Fill the preflight report and attach the previewer result.

**Outputs.** Final interior PDF and cover PDF in 07 KDP and Print (doc 09 names them; only the files actually uploaded carry `-final`), and the preflight report.

**Who.** Production does the work and signs the preflight. Heather uploads.

**Gate.** Production signs that the files pass the Stage 6 checklist and KDP's previewer. Heather's upload to the KDP draft is her yes to move on.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 6 Prepress.

### Stage 7: Proof Copy

**Purpose.** See the issue as a reader will hold it, before anyone can buy it.

**Inputs.**
- Files that passed KDP's previewer in the KDP draft.
- The Issue Brief's proof line (how many proofs, or waived).

**Steps.**
1. Heather orders the printed proof from the KDP draft. Proofs carry a "Not for Resale" watermark (KDP Help, proof copies). KDP shows the delivery estimate at checkout, and says manufacturing time changes with order volume (KDP Help, "When will my proof or author copy arrive?"). Plan 10 business days per proof round.
2. Check the proof against the Stage 7 checklist: neon builds and pink, the pale cyan, show-through on dark spreads, gutter loss, bleed, spine, 7 pt type, image sharpness.
3. Write the proof report: page, problem, fix. Fixes go back through Stage 6. A fix that changes art or text needs a Change Request.
4. If a second proof is needed, Heather decides and orders it.

**If the proof is waived.** Heather may waive the printed proof for an issue in its brief (she did for Vol. 2). Then the KDP previewer and the Stage 5 full read are the last checks, and the proof report records "waived" with the date of her decision. The Brand Standards hub says to order a proof copy for any neon build. Until Heather sets the series default, follow the hub unless the Issue Brief records her waiver (open question in 13 Open Questions for Heather).

**Outputs.** `SE-V## Proof Report`, and Heather's yes to publish.

**Who.** Heather orders (it spends money). AD and Production check.

**Gate.** Heather approves the proof, or approves publishing without one.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 7 Proof Copy.

### Stage 8: Release and Archive

**Purpose.** Publish, confirm the live book is right, and file everything so the next issue (or a corrected edition) can be rebuilt.

**Inputs.**
- Heather's yes from Stage 7.
- All final files, logs and docs for the issue.

**Steps.**
1. Heather publishes in KDP. KDP says it takes about 3 business days for a book to go live, with the Amazon.com detail page and in-stock status taking up to 72 hours (KDP Help, publishing timeline). KDP paperbacks can't take pre-orders, so the book must be live on launch day.
2. Once the book is live, check the live listing page and the look-inside preview against the final files.
3. Build the archive package in the issue's 08 Package folder (doc 09): layout source files, every placed image, the font list with license status, all art-direction docs, the art log with Midjourney job IDs, the rights log and its saved rights evidence, the final interior and cover PDFs, and a text-searchable PDF or text export of the final pages.
4. Move every older build and draft to the archive folder, marked SUPERSEDED.
5. Write the Release Note (doc 11).
6. Hold a short look-back. File what went wrong as Standards Change Requests (section 9), and add open calls to 13 Open Questions for Heather.
7. After release, corrections follow the house rule: printed in the next issue, in the same type size as the original error (Brand Standards hub), plus the public corrections process in the research standards.

**Outputs.** Live book; `SE-V## Release Note`; the archived issue package.

**Who.** Heather publishes. Production builds the archive and writes the release note.

**Gate.** Heather's publish click is the gate. Production marks the archive complete in the production tracker.

**Checklist.** 08 Quality Checklists, Stage 8 Release and Archive.

### Without a printed proof (proof waived in the brief)

Drop the proof rows. Upload at L − 5 and publish at L − 4; every earlier date moves 16 business days later. Heather chose no printed proof for Vol. 2.

### Second proof round

Add 13 business days (order, about 10 days to arrive, check and fix). Decide on a second proof at the first proof report, never later.

### How the numbers were set

- KDP go-live times come from KDP Help (publishing timeline). The proof delivery estimate comes at KDP checkout, so 10 business days is a planning number, based on the "1 to 2 weeks" the Vol. 2 plans assumed. Replace it with the checkout estimate as soon as the proof is ordered.
- Layout, review and art spans come from the Vol. 2 schedule (layout about 6 business days for about 124 pages; a 2-day full-layout read; art about 10 days alongside layout). They are planning numbers. Change them in the Issue Brief if the issue is bigger, smaller or has a new vendor.

## 7. How changes are requested and recorded

### When you need a Change Request

Use the Change Request template (doc 11) for any change after a gate has passed that:

- changes the page count, the running order or a piece's page budget;
- changes locked text (editorial owns the fix; design never edits locked text);
- changes the cover, a cover line or an approved concept;
- adds a fact, number, quote or date (it also goes to Fact-check / Research);
- swaps an image for one with a different process or rights status;
- moves the launch date or a gate date;
- spends money or adds a paid service;
- needs a color, font, logo use or format not in the Brand Standards hub.

Small fixes inside an approved item (a typo in a caption, a crop, a widow, a kerning fix, a reflow within the same pages) need no Change Request. Note them in the build's change notes.

### The steps

1. Anyone can raise a Change Request. Give it the next number for the issue (`CR-001`, `CR-002` and so on).
2. The AD triages it within one working day: what it touches, what it costs in days, which stages must run again.
3. Decide it at the right level:
   - **AD decides:** anything inside an approved concept that keeps page count, running order, cover and text the same.
   - **Editorial decides, Heather approves:** any change to locked text.
   - **Heather decides:** everything else on the list above.
4. Record the decision in the issue's Change Log (the CR list in the issue's plan folder) and in the production tracker.
5. Re-run the checklist for every stage the change touched.

### Rules

- Never apply a change before it is decided, even a "quick" one.
- A change that touches a fact always goes through Fact-check / Research.
- If Heather's answer is not in writing, it is not decided.

## 9. How these standards change

### Who owns what

- **Heather** approves every change to this set. Nothing changes without her written yes.
- **The process owner** (the Documentation Specialist for Process and Governance) keeps the set, batches change requests, and keeps 12 Decision Log and 13 Open Questions for Heather up to date.
- **Each doc's owner** drafts changes to their doc.

### The steps

1. Anyone may file a Standards Change Request (doc 11, Change Request template, type "Standards"). Say which doc, which rule, what went wrong, and the proposed wording.
2. The process owner checks it against the Brand Standards hub and 12 Decision Log. If it would contradict the hub, it goes to Heather as a hub question, never as a change to this set alone.
3. Heather approves, edits or declines.
4. On a yes, the doc owner edits the Markdown master, bumps the version, and adds a line to 12 Decision Log: date, decision, what it replaces, who approved.
5. The Drive copies and the web portal are rebuilt from the same Markdown. Every doc and page shows the same version and date.
6. The old version is kept for history, never deleted.

### Version numbers

Follow the hub's pattern (Brand Standards hub, "Approvals and versions"):

- **Whole number (1.0 to 2.0):** a rule changed. Anyone working to the old rule must be told.
- **Decimal (1.0 to 1.1):** wording, a fix, an example, or a new template that changes no rule.
- A draft is 0.x or carries "draft" in its status line until Heather approves it.

### Naming in Drive

Use the names already in use in Drive:

- The live version carries `(CURRENT)` at the end of its title, for example `01 Issue Design Process: From Brief to Print-Ready Files (CURRENT)`.
- A retired version is renamed `SUPERSEDED – <old title> (use v<N>)`, the pattern in doc 09, and moves to the set's archive folder the same day (doc 09 names the folder). Never leave it beside the live file. Never trash it.
- Title and first line always match.

### Issues already in progress

An issue past Stage 2 when a standard changes may finish on the old version, unless Heather says otherwise in the change. Note which version the issue used in its Release Note.

### Which rule wins

1. Heather's newest recorded decision.
2. The Brand Standards hub, for anything about brand: logo, color, type, voice, rights and the rules in its Decision log. If this set disagrees with the hub, the hub wins, and the set gets fixed.
3. This set, for how an issue is designed and produced.
4. The source design docs this set is built on: Design System v3: Neon Zine Print, then Design System v2 Core and Addendum A where v3 does not replace them.
5. Older docs and skills. Where a skill disagrees with the hub, the hub wins (Brand Standards hub, "Rules inside skills that may be out of date").

When two sources disagree and neither the hub's Decision log nor 12 Decision Log settles it, do not decide. Follow the hub until Heather decides, and add the question to 13 Open Questions for Heather.

## 10. What Vol. 1 taught us (and the rule each lesson made)

These come from how Vol. 1, The Loser's Issue, actually went. Each one is now a rule in this process.

| What happened in Vol. 1 | The rule now |
|---|---|
| The fact-check errata (64 items) were logged a week after the print file was final | The fact-check log closes before layout starts (section 4), and display text added in layout is checked in Stage 4 |
| The page count grew from 96 to 102 to 111 pages, and the contents order and titles changed late | Heather approves the page count at Stage 1. Changes after that go through a Change Request |
| Pieces were pulled in from other working threads during layout | Every piece is in Drive and on the flatplan before Stage 4 starts |
| The final print PDF had no text layer, and it was too big to read back from Drive, so later work could not quote the printed text | The archive keeps a text-searchable PDF or a text export of the final pages (Stage 8) |
| There was no layout package a designer could reopen; the issue was built as HTML and CSS printed to PDF | Any tool is fine, but the archive package must let someone rebuild the issue: source, images, fonts, art direction notes and approved Midjourney links |
| The Napoleon art plan listed paid museum licenses | No paid licenses. Every image has a Cleared row in the rights log before Stage 6 |
| The content calendar planned gatefolds, perforations and inserts that KDP can't print | The Issue Brief lists every item KDP can't print and its printed replacement (Stage 0) |
| Several review builds sat side by side with similar names | Builds are numbered, older builds move to the archive folder, and only the newest build is reviewed |
