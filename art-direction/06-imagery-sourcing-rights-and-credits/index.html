Imagery Sourcing, Rights and Credits

Version 1.0 draft · November 24, 2026 · Owner: Bea Archer, Art Director, Photo, Archival Imagery and Rights

Status: DRAFT for Heather's review

Who this is for: Anyone who finds, makes, treats, places or credits an image in a SIDE EYE issue: art directors, image researchers, layout designers, outside vendors and AI agents.

Use it when: You need an image of any kind, you are about to place one, you are filling in the rights log, you are writing credit lines or the colophon, or you are running the rights check before print.

Related: 01 Issue Design Process: From Brief to Print-Ready Files; 03 Page and Spread Standards; 04 Grid and Typography; 05 Art and Illustration Direction (with Midjourney Prompt Standards); 07 Print Production and Prepress (Amazon KDP); 08 Quality Checklists; 09 File Naming, Folders and Handoff Specs; 12 Decision Log; 13 Open Questions for Heather. Source docs: the Brand Standards hub (Visual identity: People in pictures, Image rights); SIDE EYE Design System v3: Neon Zine Print (Rules and rights; HYBRID); Design System v2 Core; v2 Addendum A, KDP Production Spec 8.5 x 11.

1. The rules on one card

Read these first. Every other section explains one of them.

  1. Use only four kinds of images: our own (photos, scans, lettering we made), public domain, free for commercial use, or art we generated. Never pay for a license. (Brand Standards hub, Image rights.)
  2. Every image gets a row in the rights log and a credit line. Nothing with status Pending or Blocked goes to print.
  3. Every historical image needs a confirmed public-domain or free-license basis before it is placed. "It's old" is not a basis. "No known restrictions" is not a basis on its own.
  4. If you can't confirm the rights, replace the image. No layout may depend on one image we can't clear.
  5. Illustrations carry the argument. Period images carry the proof. An illustration never imitates a period document and never sits inside one.
  6. Period art is never recolored pink or neon. It gets grayscale and coarse halftone, or full color on openers only. In HYBRID, skin is the one peach.
  7. People: generated likenesses of historical figures are allowed as caricature or real period art. Living people follow the sourced-public-facts-only rule. No image, caption or pairing may imply a crime, deal or motive the Scorecard rates below Proven.
  8. Found material shows no readable brand logos and no personal information.
  9. 300 ppi at final placed size. Nothing under 200 ppi is placed at any size.
  10. Influences are credited in the colophon only. Never name an artist, studio, shop or magazine in a prompt, caption, file name or marketing.

3.2 Never use

  • No paid sources of any kind: Getty Images, AP, Reuters, Alamy, Shutterstock, Bridgeman Images, Granger, Science Photo Library, or any picture library or museum image license that costs money.
  • "Editorial use only" images, wherever they come from. SIDE EYE is a product sold on Amazon. That is commercial use.
  • Stock photos, free or paid, including "free" stock with model releases. A model release doesn't replace our rights check. If a stock photo is the only option, use a generated image instead.
  • Non-commercial licenses (any CC "NC"), NoDerivatives licenses (any CC "ND"), and museum terms like "free for personal or educational use." In past research this ruled out the British Museum, Tate's free downloads, the Victoria and Albert Museum, Royal Museums Greenwich and the David Rumsey Map Collection.
  • News sites, social media, image search results, Pinterest, blogs, fan pages, and any image with a watermark.
  • Other people's AI images. We generate our own.
  • A copyrighted photo as a reference. Don't trace it, redraw it, describe it to Midjourney to re-create it, or upload it as an image prompt or style reference. A close copy is still a copy.

3.3 How Vol. 1 did it (the model)

  • Researchers pulled period art from museum open-access programs (the Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art, the Rijksmuseum) and archival photos and records from the Library of Congress, the National Archives, the Australian War Memorial and Wikimedia Commons.
  • Each downloaded file was named with a source key, the artist and the title (for example a Met object number, then the artist, then the title). The item's museum page address was stored in the file's description.
  • A credits sheet listed key, source, artist, title, date and museum page for every file. Its Status column tracked the download, not the rights. A downloaded file still needs its full rights check.
  • The issue printed short credits where images appear and a full page-by-page credit list at the end of the sources section.

4. Verifying rights

4.1 Do the checks in this order

  1. Open the item page at the holding institution. Not a search result, not a repost. Copy its address into the log.
  2. Read the rights statement on that page. Save a PDF or screenshot of it in 04 Art/06 Rights Evidence, named SE-V<vol>-<type><NN>_rights_<YYYY-MM-DD>.pdf (or .png), dated the day you read it (09 sets the pattern). Rights statements change. This is your evidence of what the page said on the day you checked.
  3. Find the basis. Write down why it is free: a date, a government source, a CC0 dedication or a named license. See 4.2 and 4.3.
  4. Check what the license lets us do (crop, cut out, recolor, composite). See 4.6.
  5. Check the content rules: people (section 9), found material (section 8), the Scorecard's Must NOT claim list.
  6. Check the resolution (section 11).
  7. Write the credit line (section 10) and set the status.

4.2 Date and jurisdiction tests

SIDE EYE prints in the US through Amazon KDP, and KDP sells the issue in other countries too. Run the US test on every image. Then run the life-plus-70 test.

Test Passes when Notes
US: published long ago First published 95 or more years before January 1 of this year. In 2026: published in 1930 or earlier. From January 1, 2027: 1931 or earlier. Use the publication date, not the date the photo was taken.
US: federal government work Made by a US federal employee as part of the job State and city government works are not automatically free. Neither are contractor or donor works held in federal collections.
US: no notice or no renewal Published 1931 to 1977 without a copyright notice, or 1931 to 1963 and never renewed Needs evidence. If you can't show it, don't rely on it.
US: unpublished work Creator died 70 or more years ago (in 2026: died in 1955 or earlier) Letters, diaries and private snapshots are often unpublished.
US: faithful copy of flat art A straight photo or scan of a public-domain painting, print or document The copy adds no new copyright in the US. Outside the US the law is less settled.
Life plus 70 (most other countries) The creator died 70 or more years ago (in 2026: died in 1955 or earlier), or for anonymous and company works, published 70 or more years ago If an image passes the US test but fails this one, mark it Pending and flag it. Follow this rule until Heather decides (open question).

4.4 Flat art versus objects

  • Flat things (paintings, prints, drawings, documents, maps, photographs): a faithful photo or scan of a public-domain original is treated as public domain in the US.
  • Three-dimensional things (statues, coins, medals, artifacts, buildings): the photographer of the object owns a new copyright in the photo. You need the photo's own license. A Met or Smithsonian CC0 mark covers it. A random photo of an old statue does not.
  • Modern monuments and sculptures: a photo of a statue or monument made after 1930 can be blocked by the sculptor's copyright even if the photo is free. Check the date of the object, not just the photo.

4.5 Wikimedia Commons checks

Use a Commons file only if all of these are true:

  • The file page names an original source you can open: a museum, archive, library or agency item page.
  • The source page's rights statement agrees with the Commons license tag. If they disagree, follow the source page.
  • The tag gives a real basis: a date, a government source, CC0, or a named CC license from the actual creator.
  • "Own work" uploads are photos the uploader really took, not scans of someone else's book, postcard or photo.
  • A "PD-Art" tag is on a flat work only, not a statue or object (see 4.4).
  • The file has no open deletion request.
  • A reverse image search doesn't turn up an earlier, copyrighted original.

Credit the creator and the original institution, then "via Wikimedia Commons."

4.6 What each license lets us do

License or basis Crop, grayscale, halftone Cut out, composite (HYBRID) Notes
Public domain, CC0, Public Domain Mark Yes Yes The only images allowed in HYBRID
CC BY Yes, say "cropped" or "altered" in the credit No HYBRID asks for a public-domain figure
CC BY-SA Yes, say "cropped" or "altered" in the credit, with the license name No Our altered version must carry the same license. Vol. 1 used one this way

6. Treating period art

6.1 The house finish

  • Run period art in grayscale with coarse halftone, or in full color on openers only.
  • Never recolor period art pink or neon. Pink is for SIDE EYE's own art only.
  • Never colorize a black-and-white original.
  • On QUIET pages, run it plainly: black and gray, plain halftone, wide margins.

6.2 HYBRID: a real historical person on an opener

Follow the HYBRID recipe in Design System v3. The rights side:

  1. Start from a public-domain print or painting (status Cleared, basis public domain or CC0).
  2. Cut the figure out in Photoshop or in layout. Do not upload it to Midjourney.
  3. Reduce it to black line and halftone. Add one peach (C0 M35 Y40 K0) for skin only. Nothing else.
  4. Build the scene around it in RELIEF: the hands, props and objects that make the argument.
  5. Set it on a flat deep navy or cream field. On navy, strings, threads and rules go pale cyan or pink, never black.
  6. Credit it with the composite line (section 10). The log row is type X and lists the original H image's ID in Notes.

6.3 Illustration versus evidence

  • Illustrations carry the argument. Period images carry the proof.
  • Never generate anything that looks like a period print, document, newspaper clipping, letter, seal, signature, stamp, map or museum object.
  • Never put an illustration inside a period image, and never put a period image inside an illustration's frame as if it belonged there.
  • Generated paper, ledgers, letters and signs are always "completely blank."
  • When a page needs a real document, use the archival scan. In comps, a placeholder is fine. Replace it before print.
  • Caption a generated reconstruction of a real place or object as an illustration or reconstruction.
  • No generated lettering anywhere. Every word is set in type or hand-lettered.

7. AI-generated art: the rights side

05 Art and Illustration Direction (with Midjourney Prompt Standards) owns how to make generated art. For rights:

  • Generate only from SIDE EYE's own approved style references. Never upload anyone else's artwork, a period image or a photo as a reference or image prompt.
  • Never name an artist, studio, shop or magazine in a prompt.
  • Check every generation at 100 percent before you log it: no letters or fake text, no logos or near-logos, no recognizable product designs, no unintended faces.
  • A generated person must read as caricature or stylized art, never as a photo of a real person.
  • Log every placed generated image (type I). Its credit is the single Midjourney credit (section 10).

8. Found material, our photos, scans and lettering

8.1 Found ephemera

  • Use material we made, material in the public domain (published 1930 or earlier in 2026), or plain generic material with no artwork on it (blank tickets, ruled paper, plain envelopes, stamps with no picture).
  • No readable brand logos. Turn the logo away, cover it with another layer, or crop it out.
  • No personal information: names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, account numbers, ID numbers, signatures or faces of private people. Cover or remove them before the board is photographed.
  • Photograph finished collage boards with soft side light so the paper edges show.
  • Log each finished board as one F row, with the materials listed in Notes.

8.2 Our photos

  • Shoot props, stunts and boards with the logo and personal-information rules above.
  • Letters and replies we receive in a stunt: cover private addresses, phone numbers, emails and signatures before you photograph them.
  • Credit: "Photo: SIDE EYE."

8.3 Scans

  • Scan line art, documents and hand lettering at 600 ppi grayscale.
  • Scan textures and objects at whatever gives 300 ppi at the largest size you'll place them, and never under 600 ppi for anything with fine line.
  • Save scans as lossless files. Keep the untouched master and work on copies.

8.4 Hand lettering

  • All lettering is real: set in type, or written by hand and scanned. Never a "handwriting" font. Never generated.
  • A vendor who letters for SIDE EYE hands over the scans and the right to use them (see Gaps in the notes; the contract language isn't settled).

9. People in pictures

9.1 Historical people

  • Real period art of historical figures is allowed. So are generated likenesses, as caricature or stylized art, never photoreal.
  • AI Dead figures are always skulls wearing one defining prop.
  • A person shown suffering, dead or as a victim runs plainly on a QUIET page. Never cut out, composite, recolor or joke around that image.

9.2 Living people

  • Follow the sourced-public-facts-only rule. Show only what the sources prove they did.
  • Use only free-licensed real photos (often official US government portraits, after the checks in 4.1). Run them unaltered except crop, grayscale and halftone.
  • Never composite a living person into a scene, give them props, or show them doing anything.
  • Don't generate a likeness of a living person. Follow this until Heather decides (open question).

9.3 What the art may imply

  • The Scorecard's Must NOT claim list binds images as much as text.
  • No image, caption, crop or pairing may imply a crime, deal or motive the Scorecard rates below Proven. Two people placed side by side, a face next to a crime headline, or a hand passing money to a named person all count as claims.
  • A living person on a cover, back cover or any promotion: stop and ask Heather.

10.2 Where credits go

  • On the page: a short credit in the caption (GT America Mono, set per 04 Grid and Typography) for every H and X image, or a page-by-page list if the page has no captions. QUIET captions carry no jokes.
  • The colophon carries three things: the one Midjourney credit, the image credits, and the influence credit (Brand Standards hub, The magazine).
  • The full list: Vol. 1 ran a page-by-page "Photo credits" list at the end of the back-of-issue sources section, headed: "Illustrations are generated with Midjourney and edited by SIDE EYE. Unless credited, they are not photographs of real people or events. Historical images are credited where they appear and listed here." If the list won't fit the colophon, do the same and point to it from the colophon. Where the list lives is an open question (see notes).

10.3 The Midjourney credit

One credit, at the front of the magazine, in the colophon:

Illustration generated with Midjourney and edited by SIDE EYE.

10.4 The influence credit

The colophon credits the influences once, as a credit only, never as affiliation or endorsement. Design System v3 suggests:

With admiration for the art zines and scrapbooks of 1990s Japan, the silkscreen collage of Tokyo's pop printmakers, and the letterpress show posters of Hatch Show Print.

  • Hatch Show Print appears here and nowhere else: not in prompts, captions, file names, briefs to Midjourney, marketing, the Amazon listing or social posts.
  • Never use a shop's or magazine's name, logo, typefaces cut from its blocks, or recognizable layouts.

11. Resolution, scans and upscaling

Measure effective resolution: source pixels divided by placed inches. 07 Print Production and Prepress (Amazon KDP) owns the rest of the file prep.

11.1 Specs

Item Minimum
Any placed image 300 ppi at final placed size
Hard floor 200 ppi. Nothing below this is placed at any size
Full-bleed page (8.625 × 11.25 in with bleed) 2,588 × 3,375 px
Full-bleed spread (17.25 × 11.25 in with bleed) 5,175 × 3,375 px
Line art, document scans, hand lettering 600 ppi grayscale where possible
Cover art 300 ppi

Sharpen after scaling, not before. Convert RGB to CMYK before export (07).

11.2 Upscaling limits

  • Generated art: you may upscale with Midjourney's upscaler or an AI upscaler. Vol. 1 upscaled Midjourney images 2x and then 4x. Check the result at 100 percent for invented letters, faces, fingers and mush. Re-run if you find any.
  • Archival images: download the largest file the institution offers first. Most open-access programs offer full-resolution TIFF or JPEG files.
  • If the original gives 200 ppi or more at the placed size, you may resample it with ordinary bicubic resampling to 300 ppi for output.
  • If it gives less than 200 ppi, don't place it at that size. Place it smaller, find a bigger scan, or replace it.
  • Never use AI upscaling, "enhance," face restoration or colorizing on an archival image. They invent detail that isn't in the record, and the record is the proof. Follow this until Heather decides (it's a new rule; see notes).
  • Hard floor stays: no amount of upscaling makes a 150 ppi original count as 300.

11.3 Real currency

Issues about money will show real banknotes. Show any real banknote at less than 75 percent or more than 150 percent of actual size, one side only per image. Keep the SIDE EYE Bucks page clearly different from real currency, with its disclaimer band, and send both to the counterfeit-deterrence review the Front and Back Matter doc calls for. Some image software refuses to open banknote scans; that's expected.

12. When the rights can't be confirmed: replace it

Set the row to Blocked and work down this list. Stop at the first one that works.

  1. Find the same image at another institution with a clear CC0, public-domain or free-license statement.
  2. Find a different public-domain image that proves the same point.
  3. Use the record itself: a document, report, court opinion or register entry that is public domain.
  4. Draw it in-house: a diagram, chart, map or timeline built in layout. (The Vol. 1 plan called for an in-house X-shaped diffraction diagram in place of a protected photograph.)
  5. Illustrate the idea instead of the image: a RELIEF illustration of props, hands or objects, captioned as illustration. Never a re-creation of the protected image.
  6. Cut the image and let the text carry the page.

Never: buy a license, run it "for now," use a small web copy, trace it, or ask Midjourney to re-create it.

13. The known open rights issue: the Loving photographs

Where: The Mating Issue, the feature "Same State, Same Day" (Loving). The problem: The issue plan says to "license the Loving photographs." The best-known photographs of the couple were made for a news magazine and are still in copyright. Licensing them breaks the no-paid-license rule. The Issue Hub logs this as a decision for Heather, and the Brand Standards hub lists it as an open question. Status: Blocked until Heather decides. Do not place, trace, redraw or re-create those photographs. The options on the table (Issue Hub):

Option What it means What it costs
A. Public records plus no-face generated art The court records, the Supreme Court opinion and other public records carry the proof. RELIEF or NEWSPRINT art shows objects and settings, no faces. Loses the faces readers know. Each record still needs its own rights check at its archive.
B. A clearly labeled stylized illustration A generated likeness, labeled as illustration. The couple are no longer living, so the hub allows it, but family members are, and it must not echo the famous photos. Higher sensitivity risk.
C. Drop the photo treatment Text and type only. The feature loses its main image.

The editorial team's recommended pick (posted November 24, 2026) is A. Heather has not answered yet. Until she does, plan the layout so any of the three can drop in.

14. Rights checklist

Run this per image when you log it, and again on the whole issue at the rights check before print (see 08 Quality Checklists).

Every image - [ ] It's ours, public domain, free for commercial use, or generated by SIDE EYE. Nothing paid. - [ ] It has a row in the rights log with every field filled. - [ ] Status is Cleared. No Pending or Blocked rows are placed. - [ ] It has a credit line in the right format, in the right place. - [ ] Effective resolution is 300 ppi at placed size, and never under 200.

Historical images (H, X) - [ ] The URL is the item page at the holding institution. - [ ] The rights statement is saved, with the date checked. - [ ] The basis is written down (date, government work, CC0, named license). - [ ] It passes the US test. It passes life plus 70, or it's flagged. - [ ] For a photo of a 3D object, the photo's own license is clear. - [ ] No NC, ND, "editorial use only" or "personal use" terms. - [ ] Not recolored pink or neon. Not colorized. Not AI-upscaled. - [ ] HYBRID figures are public domain, line plus halftone plus peach skin only. - [ ] No illustration imitates it or sits inside it.

Generated images (I) - [ ] No letters, logos, product designs or unintended faces. - [ ] Nothing that passes as a real photo or period document of a real person or event. - [ ] No outside artwork used as a reference or image prompt. - [ ] No artist, studio, shop or magazine named in the prompt.

People - [ ] Living people: real free-licensed photos only, unaltered except crop and tone, facts sourced. - [ ] No image, crop, caption or pairing implies anything the Scorecard rates below Proven or lists under Must NOT claim. - [ ] Victims and suffering run plainly on QUIET pages. - [ ] Any living person on a cover or in promotion is approved by Heather.

Found material and our photos - [ ] No readable brand logos. - [ ] No private person's name, address, phone, email, ID number or signature.

Issue level - [ ] One Midjourney credit, at the front, in the colophon. - [ ] Influence credit in the colophon only. Hatch Show Print named nowhere else. - [ ] The credit list matches the rights log one to one. - [ ] Known open issues (the Loving photographs) are resolved or the image is out.
