Page and Spread Standards

Version 1.0 draft · November 24, 2026 · Owner: Theo Banks, Art Director, Spreads and Page Recipes

Status: DRAFT for Heather's review

Who this is for: Any designer, art director, outside vendor or AI agent who lays out pages of a SIDE EYE issue.

Use it when: You plan an issue's page order, build a flatplan, design any interior page or spread, or check a spread before handoff.

1. How to use this document

This document tells you what goes on every page of a SIDE EYE issue, in what order, and how each kind of page is built.

  • Section 2 is the issue's running order and page budget.
  • Sections 3 to 6 are the systems that run through every page: LOUD and QUIET, filing tabs, recurring furniture and the money-page rules.
  • Section 7 has one recipe per page type. Every recipe uses the same ten rows.
  • Section 8 lists what Amazon KDP can't print and what to build instead.
  • Section 9 is the method for planning a flatplan. The blank flatplan lives in 11 Templates.
  • Section 10 is the spread-level checklist.

Rules about the grid, type sizes and styles live in 04 Grid and Typography. Rules about making the art and writing prompts live in 05. Rights and credit lines live in 06. Ink limits, file export and KDP upload live in 07. This document restates only what you need at hand while you lay out a page. Where it points to another document, that document wins on its own subject. Where anything here seems to disagree with the Brand Standards hub, the hub wins and you should flag it.

Issue numbers changed on November 24, 2026 (Heather's call). The Money Is Fake Issue is now Vol. 2 and The Mating Issue is now Vol. 3. Older documents, including The Money Pages and the Content Calendar, still use the old numbers. This document names issues by title and new number: Vol. 1 (The Loser's Issue), Vol. 2 (The Money Is Fake Issue), Vol. 3 (The Mating Issue).

3. LOUD and QUIET

Every page is one register or the other. Pick the register by subject, before you pick a process.

LOUD QUIET
Use for Comedy, myth-busting, most features, columns, Out Back Deaths, atrocities, real suffering, real harm done to real people
Paper Cream or kraft (Out Back: newsprint) Newsprint gray
Ink Black plus two inks per image. One pink focus per page Black, gray and one muted tab tint. No neon, ever. No pink
Art RELIEF, COLLAGE, WOOD TYPE, HYBRID Archival images, plain halftone photos, type. No collage, no jokes, no jokes in captions
Type Headlines may run on every axis on openers (one diagonal per spread) Horizontal only. Body 10/15 in two columns. Wide margins
Furniture Stamps, corner bars, wood-type plates, windows Gray hairlines, torn-edge dividers in gray, the tab as a muted tint
Dark fields Navy on single opener pages only An 85 percent black tint, never a solid

Rules for using the two registers:

  1. Every issue has at least one QUIET feature (Content Calendar). Vol. 2 plans three; Vol. 3 plans several, plus QUIET pages inside LOUD features.
  2. A feature can change register partway through. Change at a section break and start the new register on a new page. Example: Carthage in Vol. 1 opened LOUD, went QUIET at "How It Actually Ended," then lightened again. Several Vol. 3 features open LOUD and close on a QUIET page.
  3. When a LOUD page faces a QUIET page, print the LOUD page's RELIEF art in the cyan + orange variant, with no pink (Design System v3, RELIEF variants).
  4. Never put a joke, a pun headline or a comic spot on a QUIET page or in its captions.
  5. QUIET pages get no illustrations of the people harmed. Real people on QUIET pages appear only in real period images or public records.
  6. A generated image on a QUIET page is allowed only as a plain NEWSPRINT still life of an object or an empty place, with no people, no pink and no color, captioned "Illustration" so nobody takes it for a real record. Design System v3 says QUIET features get no illustrations but also keeps a NEWSPRINT prompt for QUIET pages, so follow this narrow reading until Heather decides (open question).
  7. Never use WOOD TYPE, COLLAGE or type on a diagonal on a QUIET page.

How to alternate. Vary density from spread to spread so turning pages has rhythm (Design System v2, Pillar 1). Section openers are the densest pages in the book. Interior LOUD pages are lighter. Group QUIET features into runs where the subject allows, and give the run a clear start and end. Do not scatter single QUIET pages between jokes.

4. Filing tabs

Each issue sorts its stories under three labels. The labels change every issue. The colors do not.

Position Color Build Vol. 1 (The Loser's Issue) Vol. 2 (The Money Is Fake Issue) Vol. 3 (The Mating Issue)
First tab Tab Red See hub print builds Lost the Fight Promised Chose
Second tab Tab Blue See hub print builds Lost the Credit Broke Wasn't Chosen
Third tab Tab Gray K48 (v2 Addendum A) Lost the Story Paid For Had the Choice Taken

Vol. 2's tabs come from its manuscript, taken as the default in its Issue Hub over the Content Calendar's older set. Confirm each issue's tabs in its Issue Hub.

What a tab means. A tab tells the reader which kind of story this is, inside the issue's theme. A piece can earn more than one tab (Carthage earned all three in Vol. 1).

Rules:

  1. Put the tab on every page of a tabbed piece, on the evidence rail at the page's outside edge. Readers are told in the contents to "look for the tab in the corner of the page."
  2. Explain all three tabs on the contents spread, with a large key (label, color, one-line meaning).
  3. Restate the tab key on The Files divider.
  4. Up Front columns, money pages, section openers, the sources section and the colophon carry no tab. Out Back columns carry a tab only when the issue plan gives them one.
  5. On QUIET pages, print the tab as a muted tint, never at full strength. Vol. 1 practice was a 30 to 60 percent tint.
  6. Map pins and timeline icons take the color of their tab.
  7. When a piece carries two or three tabs, no source says how to show them. Proposed default, not yet approved: stack the tabs on the rail in red, blue, gray order (gap, see 13).
  8. Tab Gray has no swatch in the Brand Standards hub. Use the Addendum A build (K48) until the hub adds one (gap).

6. Money-page rules

The money pages are the two front spreads, the classifieds, the last spread and the back cover. Everyone reads them, including people who put the issue down after four minutes.

  1. Make every item readable in under 15 seconds.
  2. Make every infographic one point. One chart, one idea, one kicker.
  3. Keep every block of text to 120 words or fewer.
  4. Set the kicker large, in open space, away from the chart.
  5. Label charts in mono. Keep the art to one process and black plus two inks.
  6. Set the classifieds in 7 pt mono, in two or three narrow columns with hairline rules, with no images. Place them directly before the last spread.
  7. Set any tiny legal or disclaimer line at 7 pt or larger. The Money Pages ask for 6 pt in places, but the hub's minimum is 7 pt and KDP softens type below that.
  8. Take the copy from Front and Back Matter, Vols. 1-3 (The Money Pages) or the issue's Issue Hub. Don't rewrite it in layout. Em-dashes in this copy are an open question in the hub. Set the approved copy as written and don't add or remove dashes yourself.

7. Page recipes

Every recipe has the same ten rows. "Inks" counts black plus the named inks. "Image count" is per page or per spread as stated. Word counts come from the Vols. 1 to 3 manuscripts and The Money Pages.

Rules that apply to every recipe, so they are not repeated:

  • One process per image. Never mix RELIEF and COLLAGE in one frame except by the HYBRID recipe.
  • One pink focus per page in print. If two things are pink, one is wrong.
  • No generated lettering anywhere. Every word is set in type or hand-lettered.
  • Minimum type size 7 pt.
  • Generated likenesses of historical figures are allowed. Living people follow the sourced-public-facts-only rule. Letters to the AI Dead figures are always skulls wearing a prop.
  • An illustration never imitates a period document or sits inside one. Illustrations carry the argument; period images carry the proof.
  • KDP prints on white paper, so every cream, kraft or newsprint ground is a printed flood. Bleed it off every outside edge.
  • Nothing essential within 0.5 in of the gutter.

9. How to plan a flatplan

The blank flatplan is in 11 Templates. This is the method. Work in this order.

  1. Start from the issue's lineup. Take the pieces, tabs, registers and word counts from the issue's Issue Hub and manuscript. Drop anything marked cut.
  2. Place the fixed anchors first. Page 1 (title and colophon), 2-3 and 4-5 (front spreads), 6-7 (contents), The Files divider, the map, the timeline, the Out Back opener, the sources section, the classifieds, the last spread and the last interior page. These stay in the same order every issue.
  3. Budget pages from words. Use section 2.3. Write the planned page count next to each piece.
  4. Start every spread-sized piece on a left (even) page. Every Vol. 3 feature starts on an even page, so its opener reads as a full spread. Use one-page pieces (Still Wrong, the opening letter, section openers, the Field Guide) to fill odd gaps.
  5. Set the register of every page. Group QUIET features into runs. When a LOUD page must face a QUIET page, mark the LOUD page's art as the cyan + orange variant.
  6. Pair the spreads and check each pair against these limits: - One wood-type plate per spread. - One diagonal headline per spread. - One pink focus per page. - No two navy pages facing each other. - No WOOD TYPE, COLLAGE, stamps or diagonal type on a QUIET page.
  7. Count the issue-wide limits: - One full-bleed COLLAGE feature opener per issue (the biggest feature). - One isometric headline per volume. - One typographic pun per LOUD feature at most. - At least one QUIET feature. - One A-to-Z catalog section.
  8. Check KDP. No gatefold, insert, flap, perforation or inside-cover content (section 8). Every cut-out has a sacrificial verso. Total pages even and within 116 to 132.
  9. Record each page in the flatplan template with these columns: pages, piece, recipe, process, register, tab, inks and paper, image count, headline treatment, word count, status (including OPEN and PENDING).
  10. If it runs long, cut in the order the Issue Hub lists. If it runs short, give space back to the longest features first.

10. Spread-level checklist

Run this on every spread before handoff. Every item must be a yes. 08 Quality Checklists holds the full issue-level list.

Plan - [ ] The spread matches its flatplan row: piece, recipe, register and tab. - [ ] The spread starts where planned (spread-sized pieces open on a left page).

Register and color - [ ] One register per page. QUIET pages have no neon, no pink, no collage, no wood type, no stamps, no jokes. - [ ] One pink focus per page. A LOUD page facing a QUIET page uses cyan + orange with no pink. - [ ] Black plus two inks per image (except the cover, the tabloid poster and the back-cover colorway sheet). - [ ] Period art is not recolored. Peach appears only on period cut-out skin. - [ ] No navy page faces another navy page.

Type and furniture - [ ] No generated lettering. All type set or hand-lettered. Nothing under 7 pt. - [ ] One wood-type plate and one diagonal headline at most on this spread. - [ ] Body text horizontal, on the grid, on a clean paper panel. - [ ] Tab on the rail for tabbed pieces; corner bar and coda for recurring columns; folio present.

Art and proof - [ ] One process per image. Illustrations and period images kept apart. - [ ] No AI Dead figure shown as a face. No living person shown doing something the research can't prove. - [ ] Every feature has its What We Can Prove / What We Can't box and the other side, with no stamps or badges. - [ ] Every image has a credit line or is covered by the colophon credit (06).

Money pages (if this spread is one) - [ ] Each item reads in under 15 seconds, one point per infographic, no block over 120 words.

KDP - [ ] Nothing essential within 0.5 in of the gutter. Text 0.25 in inside the trim. - [ ] Paper floods bleed 0.125 in off every outside edge. - [ ] No fold-out, perforation or flap wording. Cut-outs have a sacrificial verso.
